AI Automation Services for Small Businesses

2026-10-22

You Googled "done-for-you AI automation" at 11pm because you already know the tools exist. You've seen what they can do. The gap isn't knowledge — it's that nobody has built the thing inside your actual business and left it running. That's what this engagement delivers: scoped, integrated, operational AI automation built for your specific workflows, handed off to your team with the documentation and training to own it.

Who This Is For

Small business owners and operations managers — 5 to 50 employees — who are past the question of whether AI automation is real and stuck on why it isn't working for them yet. You're technically literate enough to have tried things. You may have a half-built Zapier workflow, a freelancer who disappeared mid-build, or a SaaS subscription you're paying for and barely using. You have at least one painful manual process you've already identified. You're not looking for a course, a strategy deck, or a consultant who emails you a prompt library. You want someone to build the system, integrate it into your existing tools, and leave it running when they go.

What's Included

Every engagement is scoped to defined workflow categories — lead handling, content production, client communication, or internal operations — and built to run, not demonstrated.

- **Workflow discovery and scoping** — your actual process mapped, including the workarounds and the places it breaks, not the version that looks clean on a whiteboard - **System build and integration** — AI automations wired directly into your existing tool stack: your CRM, your email platform, your project management setup - **Testing against real conditions** — edge cases caught before launch, not after - **Handoff with documentation and a working session** — your team operates the system from day one; the deliverable is not a Loom video - **Fixed-scope engagement** — priced per build, not by the hour, not on a retainer that keeps us necessary

How It Works

The engagement opens with discovery: mapping the workflow you want to automate in its real form, not the ideal version. That surfaces what's worth building and what needs to be fixed before automation is useful — because automation doesn't fix a broken process, it makes a broken process break faster at scale.

From there, the build goes into your existing tools. Not a sandbox. Not a prototype. The actual CRM, the actual email platform, the actual data. The system gets tested against real conditions — leads coming in at odd hours, edge cases, handoff failures — before it goes live.

When it's done, you get a working session with your team, not just documentation. The goal of the handoff is that your team runs the system the next morning without a call to us.

Engagement duration varies by scope. A single-workflow build — lead intake with automated follow-up — typically runs four to eight weeks from scoping to handoff. Combined workflow builds covering lead handling, content systems, and internal ops run longer. Scope is agreed and documented before work starts.

Why It Works

The reason most automation doesn't stick isn't the technology — it's the handoff. A system built around an ideal workflow instead of the real one fails when reality shows up. A handoff that's a document nobody opens fails the first week someone's out. This engagement is scoped to your actual workflows, built in your actual tools, and handed off in a working session your team attends. When the engagement ends, the system runs. That's not a marketing claim — it's the architecture of how the engagement is structured.

The second reason it works is accountability. There's one partner responsible for the outcome — not a freelancer who disappears, not an agency managing their own process at arm's length. The deliverable is defined, the scope is fixed, and the system either works at handoff or the engagement isn't done.

The Main Objection, Answered

**"We tried automating before and it didn't stick."**

It didn't stick because it was built by someone who handed it over without training your team to own it — or because it was built around how the workflow should work instead of how it actually works. This engagement starts with discovery that maps your real process, including the workarounds. It ends with a working session, not a document. The system is built for the technical level of your existing team: if they can use a CRM and reply to email, they can operate what gets built. That's not a concession — it's a design requirement.

If you've had a bad experience with an agency that delivered assets but not outcomes, or a freelancer who built something that's now a liability rather than an asset, that history is exactly why the operating model here is different. You're not buying a retainer. You're buying finished infrastructure you own at the end.

Terms and Next Step

Engagements are project-based, priced per scope — not hourly, not on an ongoing retainer. The discovery and scoping conversation is where scope and price get defined; no number gets attached until the workflow is mapped. If scope grows beyond what was agreed, that's a new conversation, not a surprise invoice.

Post-build, ongoing maintenance or expansion engagements are available separately. The goal of the initial build is that you don't need ongoing support for a system that was designed to run. When something does need attention — a tool update, a new workflow category, an edge case that wasn't scoped — that's a defined engagement, not an open retainer.

To start, the next step is a scoping conversation. Bring the workflow that's costing you the most time or the most revenue. That's where the build begins.
