Construction Project Health Check—Independent Review

2026-10-22

You signed a contract. The bid made sense. Then the change orders started arriving, the schedule explanations got vague, and the number on the current projection stopped matching anything you originally agreed to. You are not a developer. You do not have a construction department. And neither your architect nor your contractor is hired to tell you when something is wrong on your side of the table. That is the gap Build Watcher fills — one fixed-scope, owner-side document review, delivered as a written summary with a prioritized list of exactly what to address next.

Who This Is For

Homeowners managing a custom build, major renovation, or significant addition — people who have committed serious money to a project and are now watching costs climb, timelines slip, or change orders stack up without a clear explanation. You do not need to have a crisis. You need to have a feeling that something does not add up and no reliable way to confirm it. If you are signing payment requests without confidence, reading change orders without context, or carrying the weight of a project that feels like it is running you instead of the other way around — this review is for you.

What's Included

- Contract review. Every gap in lien waiver requirements, change order approval procedures, schedule milestones, and dispute resolution language gets named. A contract that is silent on these items is not neutral — it is a gap that benefits whoever holds the pen when something goes wrong. - Bid and scope review. The bid you accepted and the scope attached to it are the baseline. Loosely written allowances, buried exclusions, and undefined contingencies are findings — not accidents. - Pay application review. Each billing request is checked against the schedule of values and available site documentation. Billing for work not yet completed, materials not yet on site, or undisclosed markups gets flagged before you approve the draw. - Change order review. Each change order is read against the original scope. Is this work that should have been in the base bid? Is the markup rate consistent with the contract? Was it approved in writing before the work started? - Schedule review. Stated milestones are compared against actual progress. Delays are assessed against what the contract defines as excusable versus what constitutes a breach. - Written findings summary with prioritized next steps. Not a legal opinion. Not a site inspection report. A clear, direct document that names every discrepancy, identifies every missing item, and closes with a ranked list of what to address — and in what order — before you approve another payment or sign another change order.

How It Works

You submit the documents you already have — contract, bid, scope of work, pay applications, change orders, schedule, and any relevant project communications. No site visit is required to begin. Build Watcher reviews what is present against what should be present, maps the gaps and discrepancies, and delivers a written project health summary within a defined turnaround window. One fixed scope. One deliverable. One fee, published upfront — no hourly billing, no scope creep in the engagement itself.

If your documents are not fully organized, submit what you have. A missing document is itself a finding. A contract with no signed change order procedure, pay applications with no supporting schedule of values, or absent lien waivers from paid subcontractors — those gaps are part of the deliverable, not a reason to delay.

Why It Works

Every other construction management firm in your search results has a financial relationship with the build. They bill by the project, they work alongside the GC, and they have no economic reason to challenge a padded change order or reject a questionable draw. Build Watcher has one client in the room: you. The fee is fixed and the scope is fixed, which means there is nothing to gain from keeping quiet about a problem. That is not a feature — it is the only structure in which an honest review is possible. One agenda: yours.

The health check model is also bounded by design. This is not a full construction management retainer. It is a purpose-built diagnostic for the moment of doubt — a document review that turns a gut feeling into findings, and findings into a specific list of moves you can make right now.

The Objection This Answers

"My contractor is reputable — I don't think there's a problem."

A review is not an accusation. Reputable contractors still produce contracts with gaps, change orders without proper backup, and pay applications that bill ahead of completed work. Those are not bad-faith moves — they are standard industry practice, and they cost owners real money when no one is reading the documents on the owner's side. If everything in your project is clean, the review confirms it in writing and costs you a flat fee. If something is wrong, you found it before you signed the check. Either outcome is worth more than the alternative.

The same logic applies if you already have an architect. Your architect is hired to protect the design intent — not your budget, not your draw schedule, and not your contract rights. Those are different jobs. A gap in scope coverage is not your architect's failure; it is just not their assignment.

Terms and Next Step

Build Watcher is a fixed-fee engagement. The fee is scoped to the documents involved and published before you commit — no hourly billing, no open-ended retainer, no scope creep. Turnaround is defined at engagement. The deliverable is a written project health summary with a prioritized next-steps list. No site visit is required to begin.

The product is in active development; fee and engagement scope are confirmed at intake. This is construction consulting — not legal representation, not a licensed inspection service, not a guarantee of financial recovery or dispute resolution. What you get is an independent, documented picture of what your project documents actually say — and a clear list of what to do about it.
