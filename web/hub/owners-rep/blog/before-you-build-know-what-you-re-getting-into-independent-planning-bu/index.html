Before You Build, Know What You're Getting Into — Independent Planning & Budget Review

2026-10-22

You have plans, a bid, and a contract draft on your desk. Your architect reviewed the design. Your contractor priced the work. Neither one is hired to tell you where the budget is soft, where the scope is missing, or where a contract clause quietly hands schedule risk to you. That is not a failure of trust — it is a structural gap built into how every construction project is assembled. The Build Watcher Pre-Construction Project Review closes that gap before a single shovel moves — while every finding is still free to fix.

Who This Is For

This review is built for homeowners, property owners, and private project owners committing serious money — typically $300,000 or more — to a custom build, major renovation, or ground-up project. You are not a developer. You do not read construction contracts for a living. You are smart, capable, and aware enough to know that the people explaining everything to you are also the people billing you. You want one independent set of eyes, with no stake in the outcome, to read the same documents you have and tell you what is real.

If you have plans in hand, a bid on the table, and a contract draft moving toward signature — this is for you.

What's Included

The review covers everything that exists before construction starts:

- **Plans and bid scope alignment** — what the drawings actually require versus what the contractor's bid actually prices, including line items that are missing, vague, or covered by allowances too thin to be real - **Budget assumption analysis** — whether the numbers on paper track against comparable project costs or are optimistic in ways that will surface as change orders later - **Contract term review** — clauses that transfer schedule risk, cost risk, or dispute resolution to you on terms you did not negotiate, identified while you still have the leverage to renegotiate them - **Timeline and milestone gap identification** — gaps in the schedule that leave you exposed if the project slips and nobody has defined who bears the cost - **Decision gap inventory** — the questions your team has not answered yet that will cost money the moment construction starts - **Written risk register and prioritized question list** — a specific, document-level deliverable you can put directly in front of your contractor, architect, and attorney before you sign anything - **Scheduled debrief** — a structured conversation to walk through the findings so you understand what you are looking at and what to do with it

Scope, fee, and deliverables are confirmed in writing before work begins. No hourly drift. No open retainer. One project, one review, one deliverable set.

How It Works

The engagement begins with a project intake — you describe the project stage, share available documents, and confirm scope. Build Watcher reviews what exists: plans and specifications, full bid packages, budget assumptions, proposed contract terms, timeline structure, and the decision gaps your team has not flagged. The output is a written risk register and prioritized question list delivered with a scheduled debrief. From there, you take the findings to your contractor, architect, and attorney. You own the document and the leverage it gives you.

Fee is scaled to project size and document volume and confirmed at engagement — not after.

Why It Works

The pre-construction window is the only moment when every finding is still actionable at no cost. A scope gap caught before signing is a negotiation. A thin allowance flagged before contracts are executed is a conversation. A contract clause identified before your signature is on the page is something your attorney can revise. The same finding made after construction starts is documentation of a problem you now have to live with.

Build Watcher is engaged before construction starts, paid a flat fee confirmed in advance, and has no ongoing financial relationship with your contractor, your architect, or your project. There is nothing to protect, no retainer to preserve, and no reason to soften a finding. That is the only condition under which a review is actually independent — and it is the condition every other firm in this space structurally cannot meet.

The Objection Answered

**"My architect is already reviewing everything — and I trust my contractor."**

Your architect reviewed the design. Bid scope alignment, contract risk allocation, budget assumption integrity, and timeline exposure are not what architects are hired to evaluate — and when a gap costs money, it is rarely their money. Trust is not a document. Build Watcher is not about distrust; it is about knowing specifically what you agreed to so that trust has something to stand on. Good contractors welcome a prepared owner. If everything in your documents is clean, the review confirms it and costs you a flat fee. If something is wrong, you found it before you paid for it.

The question is not whether the review costs money. It is whether finding nothing cost you more.
