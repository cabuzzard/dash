Clear, Honest, Independent — Owner's Representative & Project Coordination

2026-10-22

Your architect manages design. Your contractor manages the build. Neither one is hired to protect your budget, question a pay application, or tell you a change order doesn't hold up. That gap — between what your design team covers and what your contractor is accountable for — is where money moves, scope drifts, and decisions get made without a written record. Build Watcher installs an independent coordination layer on the owner's side, before the first draw arrives, with a scope and fee structure defined in writing before work begins.

Who This Is For

Homeowners planning or already managing a custom home build, major renovation, or owner-builder project — people committing serious money who are organized and financially careful but have no construction background to fall back on. You hired an architect and a GC. You are still the least-informed person in every meeting about your own project. You have approved things you did not fully understand and you know it. You want someone whose only obligation is to you — not the contractor, not the draw schedule, not any working relationship with the subs.

What's Included

- Written scope before work begins. Deliverables, site attendance frequency, reporting cadence, and fee structure are defined and signed before the first review. No ambiguity about what is covered. - Pay application review. Every draw is reviewed against documented site progress before you approve it. If what is billed does not match what is built, you know before you sign — not after. - Change-order and decision log. Every scope change is logged with cost and schedule impact before you are asked to approve anything. Nothing is verbal-only. Nothing disappears into someone's email thread. - Budget and milestone tracking. A live record of approved changes against the original baseline so overruns surface in real time, not at closeout. - Written reporting to the owner. Regular progress reports, open-decision logs, and contractor communication run through a defined structure — not phone calls that leave no record. - Closeout coordination. Lien waiver collection, punch-list tracking, and a documented record of the project that belongs to you, not the GC.

Engagement depth — from lighter periodic advisory to more involved ongoing coordination — is set in writing at the start and can be adjusted as the project demands. Lighter engagements cover draw review and decision-log support on a defined cadence. More involved coordination includes active budget and milestone tracking, change-order review, contractor communication management, and regular written owner reports.

How It Works

The starting point is a scoping conversation to establish project size, current stage, and what coordination gaps actually exist. From there, a written scope and fee agreement is signed before any work begins. A baseline budget and schedule review comes next — establishing the written record that every future draw and change order gets measured against. Then: regular site attendance (as scoped), draw review before every approval, change-order logging before every signature, and written reporting to you on a defined cadence. Communication with contractors runs through a structured channel so everything is reviewable. The engagement runs through closeout or any defined phase, with scope adjustable if the project demands it.

Why It Works

Budget overruns in residential construction happen in four predictable ways: change orders that are legitimate but overpriced, change orders that are not legitimate at all, pay applications billed ahead of documented progress, and scope creep that accumulates through verbal approvals with no written log. Independent coordination does not prevent all of these from being attempted. It prevents most of them from succeeding — because every one of them depends on the owner not having an independent record to push back with. The written baseline, the change-order log, and the draw review are not bureaucracy. They are the thing that makes your approval mean something and your pushback hold up.

The structural fact that makes this different: every construction management firm with a relationship to the contractor's workflow — or a fee tied to a percentage of project cost — has a financial structure that makes silence cheaper than confrontation. Build Watcher represents only the owner, bills flat or by phase, and has no financial relationship with the GC, subs, or material suppliers on the project. That is not a positioning claim. It is the only arrangement in residential construction where the advisor's interest and the owner's interest are structurally the same.

The Main Objection Answered

"My GC is reputable — I probably don't need this."

Reputation is real and it matters. It is not the same thing as alignment. A reputable GC still has a financial interest in approved draws and minimal friction on change orders — that is the structure of the relationship, not a reflection of character. Your architect is hired to protect design intent; most residential architect-of-record agreements explicitly exclude budget management and contractor oversight in any substantive form. Independent coordination is not a check on anyone's honesty. It is a check on the structure — and that structure needs an independent reviewer regardless of who the GC is. If everything is clean, the engagement confirms it. If something is off, you found it before you paid for it.

Terms and Next Step

The fee and service level depend on project size, current stage, and what coordination gaps exist — that is what the initial conversation is for. There is no price assumed and no canned package; the starting point is a specific conversation about your project's actual needs.
