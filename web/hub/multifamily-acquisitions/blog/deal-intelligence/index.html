Verified Underwriting & Due Diligence Support for Multifamily Buyers

2026-10-22

You already know what you want to buy. You have a buy box, you have capital, and you have a candidate sitting in front of you that might be real. What you don't have is certainty — and at commercial scale, that gap between "this looks interesting" and "I'm confident enough to move" is exactly where acquisitions stall, or where they close badly.

Most investors who blow a multifamily acquisition don't get the math wrong. They get the assumptions wrong. Cap rate applied to a proforma rent roll nobody verified against actual leases. Vacancy set at market average without checking what the submarket has been doing for the past six quarters. Operating expense ratios lifted from a broker's offering memorandum — which was built to sell the deal, not stress-test it. The spreadsheet is clean. The spreadsheet is clean because nothing in it was ever checked against source documents.

That is the specific problem independent acquisition analysis is built to solve.

You Can't Move on a Number You Don't Trust

Decision paralysis happens in the theoretical space between what the model says and what you can actually verify. AI-generated underwriting is fast — and that is exactly the risk. The output is internally consistent. Some of the inputs are estimated from market averages because the model didn't have access to source documents. You won't know which ones until due diligence costs start and the assumptions begin to unravel.

The work product that collapses paralysis is not a faster model. It's a verified model — one where every assumption is labeled: confirmed, estimated, or stress-test variable. When you can see what each input is, where it came from, and what happens to the return if it's off by ten percent, the decision is yours to make with conviction. You're not guessing. You're underwriting.

A Finished Analytical Artifact, Not a Dashboard You Still Have to Operate

Every engagement delivers a defined work product — selected based on where you are in the process:

A Screening Report answers the first question: does this candidate hold up under initial scrutiny, or does it die here before you spend more time on it.

An Investment Memorandum goes the distance — rent roll analysis against actual leases, operating expense verification against trailing financials, comp-sourced valuation from real closed transactions, submarket context on vacancy and rent trajectory, a deal-level underwriting model with labeled assumptions, and downside cases for cap rate, vacancy, and rent growth.

A Market Comparison positions the deal against what actually closed — not against broker-selected comparables built to support the ask.

An Acquisition Shortlist delivers sourced candidates filtered against your specific buy box, including off-market and below-market inventory your competition isn't working because they're still browsing the same listed deals on the same platforms.

A Portfolio Opportunity Assessment gives you a clean picture of where value, risk, or repositioning potential actually lives in your existing holdings — before exposure surfaces as a problem.

Each deliverable is a document. Not a data feed. Not a portal you log into. A finished analytical artifact you can act on, present to partners, or carry into due diligence without rebuilding from scratch.

The Analysis Runs Against Source Documents, Not Against Other AI Outputs

Independent research means exactly that. AI-assisted underwriting is used to accelerate the work — then verified against primary data: the actual rent roll, the actual trailing 12-month operating statement, the actual tax records, the actual closed comp transactions. Not the broker's OM. Not a platform's averaged market data. Not another model's output.

Your broker's underwriting is built to close the deal. This analysis is built to tell you whether you should. Those are not the same work product, and confusing them is how assumptions that were never stress-tested end up surviving all the way to closing.

Off-Market Inventory Exists — You Just Have to Be Working the Right Data

The listed inventory is not the inventory. Motivated sellers — owners carrying deferred maintenance, managing an estate, facing a loan maturity they can't refinance, running assets at chronic high vacancy — often don't list because the asset isn't ready for a formal process. Those properties surface in tax delinquency records, expired permits, distressed occupancy signals, and loan maturity exposure. Not on the commercial MLS.

An acquisition shortlist built on this data returns candidates your competition isn't seeing, because they're working the same broker relationships and the same listed inventory. The sourcing edge is real. Off-market multifamily inventory closes before most buyers ever know it existed.

This service does that labeling.
