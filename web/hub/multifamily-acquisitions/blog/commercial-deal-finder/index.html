Multifamily Investment Opportunities — Analyzed, Packaged Deals for Qualified Buyers

2026-10-22

You know what you want to buy. You have the capital, the criteria, and the operational capacity to close. What you don't have is a pipeline of deals that actually fit — properties sourced against your buy box, analyzed before they reach you, with the investment thesis already laid out.

That's what this is. Not brokerage. Not a listing portal. Not another feed of raw inventory you have to filter yourself. Packaged acquisition opportunities: sourced, underwritten, and delivered matched to criteria you defined.

Your pipeline is the problem — not your capital

The deal flow is there. Off-market multifamily exists in secondary markets, in distressed situations, in motivated-seller scenarios that never run a full marketing process. You know this. The issue is that sourcing it is a full-time operation — direct outreach, courthouse research, relationship networks, distressed-asset monitoring — and running that operation in-house, on top of everything else your team is doing, is expensive before a single offer is made.

So you end up back on CoStar. You open the OM on a deal that forty other groups are already underwriting. You wait on a broker who sends you the same listing they sent everyone else on their preferred buyer list. The weeks pass. The capital sits.

That low-grade friction — knowing exactly what you want and still not being able to source enough of it — is the problem this solves.

What lands on your desk when a package arrives

Every opportunity delivered to you is built against the acquisition criteria you submitted. Nothing gets sent that doesn't fit. What a qualified package includes:

Financial analysis built on actual in-place income — net operating income, debt service modeling, and return metrics run under base, conservative, and stress scenarios. Not the seller's pro forma. Not stabilized projections presented as current performance. What the rent roll actually supports, stress-tested before you open the file.

Comparable sales from the specific submarket — not metro-wide averages, not directional estimates. Comps pulled and benchmarked against the actual asset in the actual neighborhood.

Identified risk factors — deferred maintenance flags, rent roll gaps, vacancy drivers, anything that changes the underwriting story before you spend an hour of your team's time on it.

Value-add and repositioning thesis — where the upside lives, what it costs to unlock, how long it takes, and what the exit looks like when the work is done. Not "there's potential here." A specific thesis with a cost and a timeline.

A recommended acquisition strategy — how to approach the deal given the seller's situation, the thesis, and current market conditions.

The buyer's job is to verify or challenge the thesis. Not to start from zero.

How the sourcing works

You submit your acquisition criteria once: asset class, target markets, deal size, return thresholds, financing assumptions, hold period. That buy box becomes the filter. Properties are sourced against it — off-market situations, below-market opportunities, distressed apartment buildings where the problem is operational rather than structural, motivated sellers who haven't listed and aren't planning to.

Every sourced property is screened against your criteria before any analysis begins. What passes gets packaged: AI-assisted financial modeling, comp analysis, risk documentation, and a written investment thesis. The packaged opportunity lands in front of you ready to act on. The sourcing and initial underwriting work lives outside your operation. Your team's time goes to the decision, not the digging.

If the criteria are too narrow to source against reliably, that conversation happens upfront — not after weeks of silence and nothing delivered.

Why this is a different lane than your broker relationships

Brokers work their own inventory and their own buyer relationships. They send you deals that fit their book, and they send the same deals to the other forty groups on their preferred buyer list at the same time. By the time it reaches you, the OM has already been shopped.

This sources against criteria you submit, packages the analysis before the deal reaches you, and delivers an investment thesis rather than a listing link. No broker does that as a deliverable. The two are not substitutes. Your broker relationships stay where they are. This fills the gap they don't cover: off-market sourcing and pre-packaged underwriting, matched to what you've already told the market you want to buy.

"How do I know the analysis is accurate?" Every package discloses assumptions, data sources, and comp methodology. You are not being asked to trust a pitch — you are being handed a working model to verify or build on. The methodology is transparent because the point is to give your team a head start, not to sell you on a number.

"What if nothing fits my buy box?" Nothing gets sent that doesn't fit. A narrow buy box means fewer packages, not mismatched volume. If the criteria produce no sourceable deal flow, you will know that upfront — not after weeks of receiving leads that miss the mark.

The next step is straightforward

Pricing and transaction compensation are disclosed at intake and structured to comply with applicable real estate licensing requirements — not buried, not vague. Disclosed in full before any deal engagement. This is a deal-sourcing and packaging arrangement, not a subscription. Delivery is ongoing and criteria-matched; packages arrive as qualified opportunities are sourced and analyzed against your buy box.

Capital that is not working has a cost. Submit your buy box — asset class, target markets, deal size, return targets, financing assumptions, hold period — and let qualified deal flow come to you already filtered, analyzed, and ready to act on.
