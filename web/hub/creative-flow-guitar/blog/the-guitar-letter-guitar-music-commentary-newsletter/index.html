The Guitar Letter — Guitar & Music Commentary Newsletter

2026-10-22

You notice the room mic on a 1973 recording. You can feel when a tone is right before you can explain it technically. You've mostly stopped reading music media because it doesn't speak to you — algorithms reward takes, not craft, and most commentary treats its audience like they just discovered the genre. The music hasn't changed. The conversation around it has gotten shallower, and you've been listening alone.

The Guitar Letter is a free newsletter for people who hear the way you hear. Not a tips column. Not a gear roundup. Not a beginner's guide to anything. A practitioner writing down what he notices — about tone, about song construction, about the recordings that actually hold up — and sending it to the people who already know what they're listening for but rarely find anyone naming it precisely.

The gap you've been sitting in

You follow YouTube channels for the playing. But there's no place that carries that conversation further — no follow-on reading, no depth between uploads. You sense there's more to what makes a recording or performance work, but nobody is naming the actual mechanics: the mix, the narrative arc, the call-and-response that turns a song into something you feel. You're surrounded by people who don't share the obsession. The listening stays solitary.

Most newsletters in the space either sell gear or serve beginners. Nothing is written to someone who already knows the difference between a good tone and a great one.

What changes when you have a practitioner's voice in your corner

You get a reliable signal in a noisy feed. One short read that respects your time and your level. You hear the mechanics behind what you already feel — why that recording works, what the bass is doing, where the narrative turn in the song actually lives. You stay connected to new recordings and videos without having to hunt for them. And over time, you build a relationship with a real point of view that develops issue to issue — not a brand voice, not a content calendar.

The music doesn't change. Your relationship to it does. It gets more precise, more felt, and less solitary.

What arrives in your inbox

Each issue is built from four things.

One short personal observation about music or the creative life — the kind of thing a working guitarist notices that nobody else is writing down. One guitar or recording insight grounded in feel and mechanics: not theory diagrams, not gear porn, but something tactile — how a sound gets made, why a particular mix lands, where the narrative turn in a song actually lives. One musician recommendation or close commentary — a look at someone worth paying attention to, or a deeper read on someone you already know. One direct link to the latest recording, session, or video, so you stay connected to new work without hunting for it.

No paywall. No commitment required to start.

Built from the same ear as the sessions

The YouTube channel is already doing this work in video form. Every video is a proof of concept for what the newsletter delivers in written form — specific recording and tone observations you can verify with your own ears. The newsletter extends that conversation into a quieter, more precise register: the place where an observation can take a sentence or two to breathe instead of needing to hold a camera's attention.

The practitioner behind it plays, records, and engages with the material in real time. This is not commentary from the outside. Watch a few videos. If the way the music gets talked about matches how you hear it, the newsletter will too.

The one hesitation worth answering plainly

*"I already follow too many newsletters and don't read most of them."*

This one is short by design. One observation, one insight, one recommendation, one link. It respects that you do not have time for another content obligation. If it doesn't land in the first read, unsubscribe — no friction, no re-engagement sequence. The newsletter earns your attention issue by issue or it doesn't. That's the only arrangement that makes sense.

And if you're wondering whether the voice is for you: the channel is already there. If the ear matches, subscribe. If it doesn't, that's fine too.

Free. No catch at the front door.

The Guitar Letter costs nothing to start. No paywall at entry, no commitment, no trial period that flips to paid. Subscribe with your email and the next issue comes directly to you.

Yes, there are products in the ecosystem and eventually a paid tier. They're not hidden. But the newsletter earns that relationship by delivering something real first — every issue, before asking for anything. That's the deal, stated plainly.
