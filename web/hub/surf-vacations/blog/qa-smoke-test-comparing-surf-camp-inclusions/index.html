QA smoke test — comparing surf camp inclusions

2026-10-21

Comparing surf camp inclusions means reading what is actually in the package — accommodation, lessons, equipment, meals, transfers — and separating those confirmed items from optional extras that show up at checkout. Do that once, clearly, and you stop booking blind. This guide walks through a practical checklist for doing it right. ---

Why Inclusions Are the Whole Game

The price you see first is rarely the price you pay. Two camps can sit at the same headline rate and deliver completely different trips once you account for what each one actually covers. One bundles airport transfers, daily lessons, all meals, and a board. The other charges separately for every one of those.

That gap is not a fine-print trick. It is just how the category works — and if you are not reading itineraries side by side with the same criteria applied to each one, you are comparing numbers that do not mean the same thing.

Inclusions determine the real cost. They also determine what your day actually looks like on the ground.

---

The Comparison Checklist

Before you book anything, run every candidate package through the same list. Record what is confirmed included, what is listed as optional, and what is not mentioned at all.

**Accommodation** - Room type and occupancy (private, shared, dormitory) - Number of nights covered by the package price - Whether the first and last nights are included if transfer days are involved

**Surf Lessons and Coaching** - Number of sessions per day and per week - Whether coaching is group or individual, or whether both options exist - Skill level the camp is designed for — beginner, intermediate, advanced — and whether they distinguish honestly between those categories or apply them loosely

**Equipment** - Board rental: included, or an add-on - Wetsuit and leash: included, or hire cost extra - Whether equipment is shared pool or assigned to you for the duration

**Meals** - Which meals are covered: breakfast only, half board, full board - Whether drinks are included or billed separately - Dietary accommodations — confirm directly with the operator if this matters to your trip

**Transfers** - Airport pickup and drop-off: confirmed included, or arranged separately at your cost - Transfers between accommodation and surf breaks: included in daily operations, or an added cost per session

**Guide and Instruction Staff** - Ratio of instructors to guests — this affects the quality of your feedback in the water - Whether the surf guide is also the accommodation host or a separate hire - Language of instruction

**Photography and Video** - In-water or drone footage: included in some packages, a paid extra in others - Worth checking before arrival if this is something you want

---

Distinguishing an Inclusion from an Optional Extra

An inclusion is something the operator has confirmed is part of the package at the listed price. An optional extra is something you can add — at a separate cost — or decline. The distinction matters because optional extras are often not visible until you are deep into checkout or, occasionally, until you arrive.

Practical signals that something is an optional extra rather than an inclusion:

- The itinerary uses phrasing like *available*, *can be arranged*, *upon request*, or *at additional cost* - The item appears in a benefits list but is not reflected in the detailed daily schedule - The price listed on an aggregator does not match the operator's own site once you add the same extras

If the itinerary is ambiguous, that is the moment to contact the operator directly and ask for written confirmation. Do this before you transfer any payment.

---

Reading the Actual Itinerary

A headline features list is not the same as an itinerary. The features list tells you what the operator wants to emphasise. The itinerary tells you what your days look like.

When you read an itinerary, look for:

- **Daily structure** — when does surfing happen, how long, how many sessions - **Day one and last day** — are they partial days with no coaching, just travel? That affects the value per night significantly - **Free days or non-surf days** — built in by design, or gaps caused by tides, weather, or scheduling - **What happens when conditions are bad** — some operators have an alternative plan; others do not

If there is no itinerary available, ask for one before you book. A legitimate operator can produce it.

---

Comparing Equivalent Packages

Side-by-side comparison only works if you are applying the same criteria to each listing. A framework that works:

1. Fix your travel window and number of nights 2. Identify your actual skill level — be honest about this; it affects which camps are suitable and which are not 3. Record confirmed inclusions for each package using the checklist above 4. Calculate the real per-night cost once optional extras you would actually use are added back 5. Note anything each itinerary does not mention — absence of information is itself useful data

Comparing three packages this way takes longer than scanning headline prices. It also means you are not surprised when you arrive.

---

What This Test Does Not Cover

This is a QA fixture for comparing inclusions on existing listings. It does not supply:

- Current prices or availability — these change; check live - Personal experience of any operator or destination - Guarantees about operator conduct, camp quality, or conditions - Recommendations for specific camps over others

Everything here is a framework for reading what operators have already published. Verify current terms, inclusions, and conditions directly with the operator or through the booking platform before you commit.

---

A Note on Skill Level Labeling

Surf camps use beginner, intermediate, and advanced loosely. One operator's intermediate assumes you can paddle into unbroken waves and ride along the face. Another's assumes you have been in the ocean twice.

Before booking, confirm what the operator means by the level they have assigned to their camp. Ask specifically:

- What can a guest at this level do when they arrive? - What can they expect to do by the end of the week? - What happens if a guest arrives outside that range?

This is not a test question — it is a practical one. The answer tells you whether the camp is genuinely matched to where you are.
