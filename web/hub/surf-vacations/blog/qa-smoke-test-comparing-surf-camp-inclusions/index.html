QA smoke test — comparing surf camp inclusions

2026-10-21

Comparing surf camp inclusions means reading what is actually on the itinerary — not what the headline implies. This checklist exists to help you tell a genuine inclusion from an optional extra before you hand over a deposit. Run it against any listing, in any order, before you book. ---

What "Inclusions" Actually Means on a Surf Camp Listing

Most surf camp listings use the word "included" loosely. Equipment included. Meals included. Transfers included. The words are there. What they cover is another question entirely.

Equipment included almost never means a full quiver of boards matched to your ability. It usually means access to a shared rack of foam boards in a small range of sizes. If you want a shortboard, a fish, or anything performance-shaped, that is typically a rental fee on top of your package price.

Transfers included often means airport to camp, one way, on arrival day. It rarely means day-trip transfers to outer breaks, boat access to reef breaks, or the return journey on checkout day unless the listing spells that out explicitly.

Meals included is the one that surprises people most. Breakfast and dinner at camp is a common configuration. Lunch is frequently not covered, which matters because if you are surfing two sessions a day you are going to want food between them. Read the itinerary line by line and count the actual meals listed.

The mechanism here is not dishonesty. It is category shorthand. Operators use compressed language because comparison tables have limited space. Your job as a booker is to expand that shorthand back into real terms before you commit.

---

How to Read an Itinerary Before You Read the Price

Start with the daily schedule, not the inclusions bullet list. A daily schedule tells you how many surf sessions are actually programmed. A full-program camp typically runs two sessions per day — one morning, one afternoon — with coaching attached to at least one of them. What a first-time camp traveler often imagines is surf-until-you-drop access from dawn to dusk. What the schedule actually delivers is structured blocks with rest, meals, and theory built in. That is not a worse outcome. It is a different one. Know which you are booking.

Next, read the skill-level requirement. Not the marketing copy above it. The actual stated requirement. If it says "intermediate to advanced" and you are riding a foamie in waist-high close-outs, that listing is not for you regardless of how good the photos look. Ability level is a primary filter, not a courtesy note.

Then read the exclusions. Most listings bury these. A clean comparison process surfaces them first because exclusions tell you what the price does not cover — and that is the number that determines whether the package is actually good value or whether it reconstructs itself at checkout.

---

The Checklist: What to Compare, in Order

Run this against every listing you are considering.

**Accommodation** - What type of room is included — shared dorm, private room, private en suite? - Is single occupancy available, and what does it cost? - Is it on-site at the camp or transferred in?

**Surf Sessions** - How many sessions per day, and for how many days of the program? - Is coaching included in every session or only some? - What is the guide-to-surfer ratio in the water?

**Equipment** - What board types are available, and can you request a specific shape? - Is a wetsuit included or rental only? - Is there a surfboard storage option for guests who travel with their own board?

**Meals** - Which meals are covered — breakfast only, half board, full board? - Is there a packed lunch option for surf days? - Are dietary requirements accommodated without a surcharge?

**Transfers** - Does the price include the airport-to-camp transfer on arrival? - Does it include the return transfer on departure day? - Are boat transfers to surf breaks included or billed per session?

**Exclusions — look for these specifically** - Travel insurance (almost never included; always required) - Surf photography and video analysis - Additional coaching beyond the standard program - Equipment upgrades beyond the base rack - Alcoholic drinks - Laundry

---

The Inclusion That Actually Makes or Breaks the Trip

Boat access. Not board quality, not meal plan, not accommodation grade — boat access to the break.

On a land-based camp, if your included break is walkable from the camp, you are surfing that break every session. If the best wave within range requires a boat, and boat transfers are not on the inclusions list, you are either paying per trip or staying on the inferior wave for a week. For intermediate and advanced surfers this is the difference between a productive trip and an expensive frustration.

On a charter or liveaboard, this question collapses — the boat is the product, and access to remote breaks is the entire point of that format. But on a land-based camp it gets skipped because it does not fit neatly into a standard inclusions table.

Ask the operator directly: what is the primary break we surf, how do we get there, and is that transport in the price?

---

Comparing Two Listings at the Same Price

Same destination, same price, different camps. Here is the order of comparison that actually surfaces the real difference.

First, daily session count. If one camp runs ten surf sessions across seven days and the other runs fourteen, that is a meaningful gap in water time regardless of how the rest of the package compares.

Second, coaching structure. Sessions with coached feedback are not the same as free surf with an instructor on the beach. Understand which one you are getting.

Third, boat access to breaks. As above.

Fourth, meal coverage across the full day. Two meals versus three across a week of double-session surfing adds up in both cost and energy management.

Fifth, equipment quality and range. A rack of one-size-fits-most foamies and a quiver with shortboards, mid-lengths, and fish for the right conditions are not equivalent inclusions even when both listings say "equipment included."

After you have compared those five, look at accommodation. Accommodation matters, but it is the thing people compare first because it is the easiest to visualize. It is rarely what makes the trip.

---

What to Do When the Listing Is Still Unclear

Contact the operator before you book. A well-run camp responds to a specific inclusions question clearly and quickly. A disorganized one does not. Operator responsiveness at the inquiry stage is a reasonable proxy for how the camp runs on the ground.

Ask in writing so you have a record. Confirm what is included, what is excluded, and what the process is if a session gets cancelled due to conditions. Good operators have a clear answer. The answer itself matters less than whether they have one.

Check current terms directly with the operator or the booking platform before completing any reservation. Prices, availability, and conditions change. This checklist gives you the framework; the operator gives you the live details.
