QA smoke test — comparing surf camp inclusions

2026-10-21

Comparing surf camp inclusions means reading what the itinerary actually lists — accommodation, lessons, equipment, meals, transfers — and separating those from optional extras before you book. This checklist walks you through how to do that clearly, so you are not unpacking surprises at checkout or on arrival. ---

Why Inclusions Are the Whole Game

A surf camp price means nothing without the inclusions list next to it. Two packages at the same price point can be completely different trips: one covers meals, airport transfers, a board, and a wetsuit; the other covers a bunk and two lessons and nothing else.

The confusion is not accidental. Operators build packages differently, and generalist booking platforms rarely surface that detail at the comparison stage. You end up on five tabs, none of them showing the same line items, trying to reverse-engineer what you are actually paying for.

The fix is a simple comparison checklist applied before you click through to any booking.

---

The Core Inclusions to Check

When you pull up a surf camp or charter listing, run it against these categories. Record what is confirmed in the itinerary, what is flagged as optional, and what is not mentioned at all.

**Accommodation** What type — shared dorm, private room, twin share, cabin on a boat? How many nights? Is it included in the headline price or billed separately?

**Surf Lessons and Coaching** How many sessions per day? Are they guided by an instructor or self-directed? Is video analysis included, or is that an upgrade?

**Equipment** Board rental included or extra? Wetsuit? Leash and wax? For boat charters: are deckboards provided or do you bring your own?

**Meals** Full board, half board, breakfast only, or nothing? Confirm which meals are covered and whether dietary requirements are accommodated. For liveaboards, meal coverage is usually total — but confirm it.

**Transfers** Airport pickup and drop-off, or are you arranging that yourself? For charters, does the price include the tender to the boat? Does it include the return leg?

**Guide and Skipper** On a boat charter, a guide who knows the breaks is not the same as a deckhand. Check whether wave selection and spot knowledge are part of what the crew provides.

---

What Counts as an Inclusion vs an Optional Extra

An inclusion is confirmed in the itinerary and covered by the listed price. You do not pay more for it at checkout.

An optional extra is available but priced separately — a surf photography session, a massage, a reef trip on a rest day. These are not inclusions even if the listing mentions them.

A common source of friction: transfers that appear in the itinerary description but are listed as 'available on request' in the fine print. If it says 'on request' or 'at additional cost,' it is not included. Flag it, price it separately, and factor it into your real total.

If something is not mentioned at all, that is the most important category. Silence is not confirmation. Contact the operator and ask directly before you book.

---

Skill Level Is an Inclusion Too

Not every surf camp suits every surfer. A camp that lists 'all levels welcome' is telling you almost nothing. An itinerary that specifies beginner-only coaching, or that requires guests to comfortably ride unbroken waves before arrival, is giving you usable information.

Treat skill-level fit as part of the inclusions review. If a camp is optimized for progression at the beginner-to-intermediate stage and you are an intermediate-to-advanced surfer looking for boat access to serious reef breaks, those are different products regardless of what the headline says.

For boat charters in particular — Maldives, Mentawais, G-Land — the assumption is that you can handle yourself in the water. Some operators list a minimum ability level. Read it. A week on a charter with breaks outside your skill range is not a progression trip.

---

Side-by-Side Comparison: How to Run It

Set up a simple grid — either a spreadsheet or a notes page — with one column per package you are comparing. Use the inclusion categories above as rows. Fill in what each listing confirms, mark gaps, and note anything flagged as optional.

What you are looking for:

- **True cost per night** once transfers, equipment, and meals are added to a base price that excluded them - **Coaching density** — how many hours of instruction or guided water time per day - **Availability** — confirmed departure dates that match your travel window, not provisional listings - **Operator transparency** — clear inclusions, clear exclusions, clear cancellation terms

When two packages land at similar real cost, the coaching quality and wave access are usually the differentiating factors. Those are harder to read from a listing and are where traveler reviews and operator reputation do actual work.

---

What to Confirm Directly with the Operator

After you have run the checklist, contact the operator about anything marked as unclear. Specific questions to ask:

- Is airport transfer included in both directions? - What happens to unused lesson sessions if conditions are poor? - Is equipment included or available to rent on-site? - What is the cancellation and refund policy if a departure is weather-affected? - What is the minimum and maximum group size on this departure?

You are not interrogating them. You are doing the due diligence that the listing should have covered. A transparent operator answers quickly and clearly. A vague answer is information.

---

Prices, Availability, and Conditions Change

Everything in a listing is a snapshot. Prices shift with season, availability, and exchange rates. Inclusions occasionally change between when a listing is written and when you book. Cancellation terms may differ from what a third-party aggregator displays.

Confirm current terms directly with the operator or through the booking platform at the point of transaction. Do not book on the assumption that a price or inclusion you saw in a review, a forum post, or an older article is still accurate.

This checklist is a comparison tool, not a guarantee. It gives you the right questions to ask and the right structure to compare answers. The answers come from current operator data and your own verification.

---

A Note on Charter-Specific Inclusions

Boat charters and liveaboards work slightly differently from land-based camps. The vessel is your accommodation, your transport, and your wave access. That bundling means inclusions tend to be more comprehensive — meals, guiding, tender transport to breaks — but it also means the gaps matter more when they exist.

Check specifically:

- **Fishing or reef trips** — often listed as experiences but billed separately - **Alcoholic beverages** — almost always an extra - **Surf photography or video** — common upsell on charter trips - **Gratuities** — some operators include a suggested tip in the inclusions language; others do not mention it

On a ten-day Maldives or Mentawais charter, optional extras that feel small in isolation add up. Price them out before you compare the headline number.
