Surf Charters: What to Look For and What to Avoid

2026-10-24

What's the single biggest mistake you see surfers make when choosing a surf charter — the one that causes the most avoidable disappointment?

Getting onto perfect surf is a logistical challenge. Good operators don't get into this line of work because it's easy. It isn't. They get into it for the love of surf.

Experience and reputation matter a lot in this industry. Look beyond the company's marketing. Find out about the operator who's actually going to be running your boat and working with you to get you to the best breaks.

Surf charters are a form of adventure. They have their great moments, but everybody involved has to work to get things right, and there's always an element of the unknown in this kind of travel.

Don't listen to somebody who's making promises they might not be able to deliver on. I'd much rather book with somebody who has real stories to tell than somebody who's selling me a billboard or a hotel pamphlet.

When you look at a charter operator yourself, what's a detail — something most surfers would scroll past — that tells you whether the boat and crew are actually worth booking?

Ask the captain how good the tender is. That's the boat you spend a lot of time in getting in and out of breaks. It makes a huge difference. Also ask them what the maintenance schedule is on the main boat. If you can't talk to anybody in the booking process that knows anything about boats, you are in the wrong place. Don't book a surfer! You need to know a lot more than the pamphlet. Don't ever go on the water with anybody until you know more about them. Surf charter trips are a fantastic dream of a lifetime. But it goes together with a life of building the dream. You as the customer should also know this. Your preparation is a huge part of making it a success. Part of your preparation is talking to other people who have made the trip and finding out about the operators and the locations on a deeper level.

The best operators are valued for their experience, and good operators tend to take care of their boats. Pay more if it means you're actually getting more. There's no reason to spend a week uncomfortable just to feel like you're having a real adventure.

A smart captain isn't going to spend their days living on a floating shanty if they don't have to. But they'd probably know how to run one if they had to!

It's really a balance. Some of the best charter boats have been working those waters for years, or they're built on designs that have already proven themselves. A newer, fancier boat isn't necessarily a better boat. What matters is how well it's maintained, how it's equipped, and whether it's suited to the places you're going.

I'd ask the captain about the boat itself. Why that particular boat? How long has it been operating in the area? How does it handle rough weather? What kind of maintenance does it get?

Good captains usually know their boats inside and out, and a lot of them love talking about them. You can learn quite a bit from that conversation.

At the end of the day, you're going out into some pretty remote places. You want a comfortable boat, but more importantly, you want a capable boat with an experienced crew. You shouldn't have to sacrifice one for the other.

What does conventional advice about surf charter research usually get wrong, or what does it leave out that you think matters more than people realise?

There are no assurances. You are signing up for an unknown adventure. The unknown is the element of surprise that makes the whole trip more interesting. If you want assurances, book the hotel with the biggest pool. That's perfectly ok if that's what you are looking for. That isn't what surf travel is. Surf travel usually offers you up more better waves than you ever dreamed of, but what it always offers you is days full of surprises. You will catch different types of fish, you will discover untravelled beaches. You will snorkel out of the way reefs. You will drop in on tiny villages off the beaten path. You really have no idea what the weather and conditions are going to give you. And, you will surf too. While I can't guarantee waves, nobody can, I can say that I usually surf until my arms fall off and I'm crying because I can't even move my body and the sun is already gone down and I just don't want to get out of the water. That's been my most common experience. But I love the crazy random stories just as much.
