You've been sold "local" before — this is what local actually looks like

Most botanical goods — flowers, plants, dried arrangements, crafts — are disconnected from their origin by design. The supply chain that moves product at volume doesn't preserve provenance; it erases it. What you get at the end is an aesthetic object with no story.

The Sunflower Acres shop runs on the opposite logic. The plants are grown here, in this climate, on this property in Concord. The dried arrangements are made from material harvested from these fields. The wreaths were made by the people who grew the flowers in them. That's not a branding claim — it's a physical fact about where the farm is and what it does. When the shop says "locally grown," there is a field you can point to.

Inventory rotates with the seasons because the growing does too. That scarcity is not a liability — it's the proof that what you're buying is real. A finite amount was grown. When it sells out, it's because the season ended, not because the distributor ran low.

Whether it survives your Concord backyard — the dry summers, the deer pressure, the specific soil of Contra Costa County — is your problem to figure out after you get it home.

Plant starts at Sunflower Acres come out of this climate. They've been grown here.
