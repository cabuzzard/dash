Experience a Garden Without Barriers — Plan Your Visit to Sunflower Acres

2026-10-22

Most flower farms are beautiful from the parking lot. The field starts about thirty feet past where the pavement ends.

Sunflower Acres in Concord, California was built the other way around — the path continues from the accessible parking space through the gate and into the sunflower rows without a step, a slope change, or a surface break. You cut your own flowers from inside the field. You reach the photo spots. The restroom dimensions are published before you ask. Your companion enters free. And none of that is a modification of the regular experience — it is the experience.

If you have typed "sunflower farm near me" and clicked through three results looking for a real answer about whether you can get into the field, this is the answer.

You Have Booked "Accessible" Before. You Know What That Usually Means.

A paved strip near the entrance. A ramp to the gift shop. The field — the actual reason you came — starts where the pavement ends, and that is where you stop.

You have done the pre-visit call. You have asked the specific questions: What is the surface? How steep is the grade? What is the restroom turning radius? Does my companion pay? And you have gotten the general answer — "Oh yes, we're very accessible-friendly" — that tells you exactly nothing about whether the day is going to work.

The cumulative weight of planning every outing around a worst-case scenario is real. Scouting in advance, calling ahead, arriving early, leaving early, managing someone's disappointment when the venue falls short. Every accessible outing runs through the same checklist. It is exhausting in a way that has nothing to do with the outing itself.

What Changes When the Access Is Structural

Sunflower Acres was not made accessible by adding a ramp near the entrance. The access is in the infrastructure — the paths, the cutting stations, the restroom, the parking — because that is where the problem actually lives.

What that means in practice:

The path is step-free from the accessible parking area through the fields. Not selectively. End to end. Surface types, path grades, and widths are documented and available before your visit so you can check them against your actual equipment, not find out on arrival.

The cutting stations are at seated height, inside the rows. Not at a modified table near the parking lot. Not a pre-cut option offered as a workaround. You go into the field and you cut from the same station as everyone else.

The accessible restroom dimensions are published. Turning radius stated. You assess it against your real requirements before you travel.

Loaner wheelchairs are on site. Available without a prior request after you have already arrived and found the terrain difficult.

Your companion enters free. The person helping you is not a line item on the cost. That is stated explicitly, not buried in a general welcome.

A venue that has actually solved the problem can name these things. A venue that has added a ramp to a gift shop cannot.

What You Can Do on a Visit

The farm is not a background photo op. The core experiences:

U-pick cutting — you go into the field, you choose your stems, you cut. The same rows, the same stations, for every visitor.

Ready-made bouquets and dried flower arrangements — available for visitors who want something lasting without cutting, or who are visiting outside the fresh-cut peak.

Floral design workshops — hands-on, seasonal. Seating and workstation access are part of the setup. Confirm availability and configuration through the inquiry process.

Group visits — the full property can be reserved for care facilities, senior centers, disability organizations, school groups, and private events. Group bookings include access documentation up front, a direct coordinator contact, and arrival logistics confirmed before anyone travels.

A casual visit runs roughly one to two hours. Group programs and workshops run longer; timing is confirmed at booking.

For Group Coordinators: What the Inquiry Covers

If you plan outings for a senior center, care facility, or disability organization, you carry personal liability for whether the venue actually works. You cannot afford to guess on an access claim, and you should not have to conduct an in-person site visit to vet a venue before committing a group.

The group inquiry process at Sunflower Acres is where access requirements are confirmed before anyone travels. What that first exchange covers:

Date and capacity confirmation. Surface types, path grades and widths, restroom turning radius — provided in writing, not described generally. Loaner wheelchair availability. Companion and aide admission policy confirmed. Activity programming options. Fees and terms confirmed per booking. Routing and parking logistics for adapted vehicles.

The access documentation is specific enough to check against real mobility requirements without guessing. Group coordinators who have previously had to drive out and walk a venue before committing a group have found it sufficient to vet Sunflower Acres remotely.

The Ordinary Good Day

The goal of a well-planned outing is not to feel grateful that you were accommodated. It is to show up, cut some flowers, get dirt on your hands, take a photo in a field, and drive home. That ordinary experience — the unremarkable good day — is the hardest thing to find when you are planning around mobility needs.
