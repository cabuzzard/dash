Sunflower Acres Accessible Garden Fund — Donations & Sponsorships

2026-10-22

You've done this before. You found an outdoor destination that called itself accessible, did the research, made the calls, loaded the car — and arrived at a paved path to a viewpoint. The field was still somewhere else.

The Sunflower Acres Accessible Garden Fund does not work that way. Every project in the fund is scoped before it is listed — named scope, target budget, receiving organization, and a progress tracker that is visible before and after you give. You are not funding an accessibility initiative in general. You are closing a named line item. When it closes, you can drive to the farm in Concord and confirm what was built is still there, still working, still the same day for everyone.

What the "Accessible" Label Usually Costs You

Most outdoor spaces that use the word "accessible" are accessible to the edge. A paved path near the gate. A ramp to a locked door. A bench with a view of the field you cannot enter. Well-meaning donors have put money into general accessibility funds and never seen a report. Community organizations have co-signed sponsorships that were never built. Families have prepared for the outing not to work because it almost never works.

That exhaustion — the low-grade distrust, the advance negotiations, the preparation for disappointment — is not a personal problem. It is what happens when "accessible" is a label instead of a measurement.

One Farm That Published the Numbers

Sunflower Acres in Concord is step-free end to end. Not step-free to a nearby bench. Into and through the fields — the same paths, the same cutting stations, the same workshops, the same photo spots. The farm publishes the actual numbers: path grades, surface types, path widths, restroom interior dimensions, loaner chair availability, free companion entry policy stated without fine print. Those numbers are on the farm's own site. You can drive to Concord and verify them before you give a dollar to the fund.

The Accessible Garden Fund extends that same standard to future improvements. Every project is what the farm already is: measurable, specific, and physically verifiable.

Your Contribution Has a Physical Address

Individual donors can contribute at any amount toward any listed project. The project page shows the scope, the target, the current progress, and the receiving organization — before contributions open, not in a confirmation email afterward.

Community organizations in Contra Costa County and the broader East Bay can use the fund's pre-scoped project documentation in their own grant and fundraising conversations. The scope, cost, and receiving organization are already defined. You do not have to build the case from scratch. A named project can go into a board presentation or a grant application without starting at zero.

Local businesses with CSR budgets can submit a named sponsorship inquiry tied to a specific infrastructure project — a seated cutting station, a path improvement, an accessible restroom upgrade. The commitment tracks to a deliverable, not a calendar year or a renewal cycle. When the project closes, your customers, employees, and community members can visit the farm in Concord and see what was built. That is what local CSR visibility looks like when it is not a banner at a gala.

What Gets Built and How You Know

Named projects in the fund cover the infrastructure that makes the farm's step-free experience permanent and extendable: compacted path improvements, seated-height cutting stations, accessible restroom upgrades, visitor access enhancements. Each project is scoped and costed before a single contribution is accepted.

Progress is tracked against the published budget as contributions come in — not summarized once a year in an impact report. When a project closes, the farm is open. Drive out and roll or walk through what your contribution funded. There is no other accessible outdoor destination in the East Bay where that verification step is available. There is almost none in the country.

The One Question Every Cautious Donor Asks

"How do I know the money actually reaches the project?"

The receiving organization, EIN or fiscal sponsorship documentation, and payment route are disclosed before any project listing goes live — not on the way out of the checkout flow. You see the target budget before you give. You see the progress tracker after. The fund does not accept contributions against a vague or unspecified use. You are completing something that has already been specified and costed, through a named organization you can look up before you commit.

If you are vetting the farm before you vet the fund: path grades, surface types, restroom dimensions, loaner chair availability, and companion entry policy are published with specific numbers. Go to the farm. Check in person. That option is always open.

The Straightforward Confidence You're Looking For

After: a contribution with a named project, a closed budget line, and a physical destination in Concord you can return to and confirm is still there, still working, still the same day for everyone.
