Most accessibility funds ask you to give first and trust the outcome later. The Sunflower Acres Accessible Garden Fund works the other way around. Every project is defined — scope, target budget, receiving organization, progress tracker — before contributions are accepted. You give to a line item, not a concept. When the project closes, you can drive to the farm in Concord and see what was built.

Who It's For

This fund is for three kinds of people who are tired of vague accessibility promises.

Individual donors and families who've watched someone they care about get the lesser version of every outing — the paved overlook instead of the field, the waiting area instead of the workshop. They want their contribution to land on something specific enough to verify, not disappear into a general fund.

Community organizations in Contra Costa County and the broader East Bay that serve people with mobility disabilities and need a concrete, already-scoped project to present to their own funders. The case is built. The scope is documented. You can fold a named project into a grant application or a board presentation without starting at zero.

Local businesses with CSR budgets looking for a place-based sponsorship their customers, employees, and community members can actually visit. Not a logo at a gala. A working destination in Concord — a path, a cutting station, an accessible restroom upgrade — that stays there, still working, long after the check clears.

What's Included

- Project listings with full scope disclosure — each improvement is defined with a named scope, target budget, and receiving organization before any contribution is accepted - A progress tracker visible before and after you give, so you can follow the line item to close - Named sponsorship inquiries for businesses and organizations, tied to specific infrastructure — not a calendar year or a renewal cycle - Pre-scoped project documentation available to community organizations for use in their own grant and fundraising conversations - The farm itself as a proof point — path grades, surface types, restroom dimensions, loaner chair availability, and companion entry policy are published with specific numbers and physically verifiable

How It Works

You start at the project listings. Every item shows you what is being built, what it costs, who receives the funds, and where the progress tracker stands. If you are an individual, you contribute at any amount toward any listed project. If you are a business or organization, you submit a sponsorship inquiry tied to the specific project you want to fund. Contributions are not accepted until scope, budget, and receiving organization are confirmed. When the project closes, progress is reported against the published budget — and the farm is open for you to visit and confirm the result in person.

Why It Works

The farm's existing infrastructure was built on the same model: measurable, specific, and physically verifiable. Step-free paths with published grades, seated-height cutting stations, accessible restrooms with listed interior dimensions, loaner wheelchairs on site, free companion entry stated without fine print. That is the operating model, not a promise — and the fund applies the same standard to every future improvement. Specificity is not a marketing angle here. It is the reason a cautious donor gives at all.

No comparable sponsorship target exists in this geography. There is no other step-free u-pick flower farm with published path grades, restroom dimensions, and loaner chairs in the East Bay. Accessible outdoor experiences in this region are almost always a paved edge near the thing. Sunflower Acres is the thing itself. The fund is what keeps it that way and extends it as the farm grows.

The Main Objection Answered

"I don't know if the money actually reaches the project."

Every project in the fund lists the receiving organization, target budget, and progress tracker before contributions open — not in a confirmation email afterward. You are not funding an accessibility initiative in general. You are closing a named line item against a defined scope. When it closes, the farm is open. Drive to Concord and walk or roll through what was built. That is the verification step, and it is available to every contributor.

Terms and Next Step

Contributions are donor-selected; there is no minimum. Sponsorship amounts and project budgets are defined per project and disclosed before any commitment is made. Contributions are accepted only through a verified receiving organization — the organization name, EIN or fiscal sponsorship documentation, and payment route are confirmed before the project listing goes live. Tax deductibility depends on the receiving organization's status; retain your acknowledgment letter and consult your tax advisor for your specific situation.
