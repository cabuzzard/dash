The Part Nobody on the Forums Knows

Every forum thread about dirted tanks eventually gives you the same thing: a crowd of people who don't know your tank. They don't know your dimensions, your light source, your tap water chemistry, or what you're actually trying to stock. The answers are generic because they have to be. You come away with more variables and the same unresolved question: will this actually work for mine?

That question has a real answer. It just requires someone who knows the method and knows your setup — not one or the other.

What You've Already Tried Isn't the Problem

You're not behind. The research spiral is the normal experience of trying to learn a biological system from sources that were never designed to teach it. Affiliate sites are incentivised to sell you a canister filter. YouTube channels optimise for watch time, not setup accuracy. Local fish store staff are selling from inventory. Almost every conventional aquarium resource has a commercial stake in the weekly water-change treadmill — which means almost none of them have any reason to teach you a method that eliminates most of that spend.

The Walstad method reduces or removes the need for most of what the mainstream aquarium industry sells. That's why it's taught badly, and why you're still uncertain after hours of reading.

A Tank That Runs on Biology, Not Willpower

Here's what the system actually looks like once it's running: nutrient-rich soil beneath an inert cap feeds the plant roots directly. Dense plant growth outcompetes algae for those nutrients and processes fish waste before it accumulates. Stocking matched to what the ecosystem can carry means the biological loop closes cleanly. The water column stays clear not because you're removing waste on a schedule, but because the ecosystem is consuming it.

That's the after-picture. A soil-based planted system you set up once with the right inputs, watched establish itself, and now largely leave alone — a living object in your home that runs on biology rather than a weekend maintenance schedule.

Getting there requires three variables to be correct for your specific setup: the substrate, the plant selection, and the bioload. Get all three right and the system does the rest. Miss one — the wrong soil, the wrong light-to-plant match, stocking that exceeds what the ecosystem can carry — and you're rebuilding a sealed substrate you can't easily reset.

Your Substrate Spec, Plant List, and Stocking Plan — Built for Your Tank

The consultation takes your actual inputs — tank dimensions, available light, local water parameters, and intended stocking direction — and returns a written plan you can build from.

What's in the plan:

Substrate specification — the right soil type, sourcing guidance, layer depths, and cap material for your container. The single decision most beginners get wrong, answered before you buy anything. An ammonia spike from slow-release fertiliser pellets in the wrong potting mix is how people rebuild sealed substrate systems six months in. This prevents that.

Plant selection — a short list of species that will root aggressively and compete with algae under your specific light level and tank size. Not which plants look good in someone else's photos — which ones will actually work in yours.

Stocking sequence — fish and invertebrate choices matched to your bioload ceiling, with an introduction timeline that lets the biological loop establish before it's taxed.

Curated gear recommendations — substrate, lighting, and filtration picks tied to your specific plan. The operator names what they actually run and why, with no stake in selling you equipment you don't need.

Remote diagnosis access — when something looks off after setup, you send a photo and get a named mechanical cause. Not a forum thread. Not a generic troubleshooting checklist. A read on what your tank is actually doing, from someone running the method on live soil right now.
