Resident Caretaker & Home Watch — Private Property Care

2026-10-22

You own a second home — a Sierra cabin, a wine-country estate, a coastal property used three months a year. And the honest answer is that nobody reliable is watching it right now. Not a person who already knows the place, not someone who will notice the slow leak before it becomes a ceiling, not anyone accountable for what happens between your visits. This is that person. One named individual, one agreed scope, one property at a time.

Who It's For

Second-home owners, estate holders, and frequently absent homeowners in Northern California — mostly 45–70, financially established, with more property than they can actively manage from a distance. You are not looking for a property manager with a portfolio and a commission structure. You are not looking for a house-sitter from an app. You want one person you can call who already knows the house, knows what belongs there, and will be honest when something is wrong.

What's Included

The scope is agreed per property, in writing, before work begins. Base service covers:

- **Scheduled property checks** — interior and exterior walk-throughs at agreed intervals, with a written report after every visit (not a text saying "all good") - **Grounds and seasonal oversight** — lawn maintenance, irrigation management, storm debris, and anything tied to the property's seasonal calendar - **Seasonal opening and closing** — winterizing, startup prep, storm readiness, and a documented condition report so you know what you're arriving to - **Contractor coordination** — when licensed trade work is needed, the caretaker sources the vendor, provides site access, and oversees the work on your behalf; you are not project-managing from another state - **Small repairs** — sub-threshold work within California's unlicensed handyman scope (jobs under roughly $1,000, no permit required) handled before they reach contractor scale - **Pet care** — where it fits the arrangement, provided by someone who knows the property and the animals, not a rotating stranger

Resident or visit-based structure is matched to what the property actually requires.

How It Works

The engagement starts with a direct conversation and a site visit. The caretaker walks the property with you, understands the systems, the quirks, the seasonal exposures. Scope, visit frequency, reporting format, emergency contact protocol, and any spending authorization are agreed in writing before a key changes hands. From there, the property is on a set schedule — checks happen, reports come in, small problems get handled, and anything requiring a licensed trade contractor gets escalated and coordinated on your behalf. You stop managing it. You start receiving updates.

Pricing is paid, work-trade, or a documented hybrid — determined by the property and the agreement. No packaged tiers, no management percentage, no portfolio model.

Why It Works

The gap this fills is real and not small. Licensed contractors won't schedule work under a few thousand dollars — the small stuff never gets done. House-sitting platforms send unvetted strangers with no repair skills and no accountability structure. Property managers are built around rental income and tenant relations; they are not built for the absent owner who wants the property maintained, not monetized. None of those options produce what you actually need: one trustworthy local person with real skills, a defined scope, and a referral-first reputation who already knows the house before anything goes wrong.

One person who knows the property is categorically different from a dispatch system. Familiarity is not a soft benefit — it is the mechanism that catches a failing pressure regulator before it becomes an emergency, notices the pest entry point before September becomes March, and calls you with a specific assessment rather than a vague heads-up.

The Main Objection Answered

**"I don't know you."** That's correct — and that's the starting point, not a problem to talk past. This arrangement begins with a referral or a direct conversation, a site visit, and written terms before any money or access changes hands. You will know exactly who is on your property, what they are authorized to do, and what they are responsible for reporting. The scope is honest about what falls within unlicensed handyman work and what requires a licensed contractor. When trade work is needed, it is sourced, scheduled, and overseen — you are not left coordinating from a distance with a contractor who doesn't know you. The agreement is in writing. The person is accountable. That is the whole structure.

Terms and Next Step

Scope and terms are agreed per property — there is no packaged price because the arrangement is built around what the property actually requires. Visit frequency, specific responsibilities, reporting format, and any resident or work-trade component are set before work begins. No portfolio model, no rotating staff, no management percentage.

To discuss your property's specific situation, what a realistic scope of care looks like, and whether a visit-based or resident arrangement fits what you need — reach out directly. The first step is a conversation, not a contract.
