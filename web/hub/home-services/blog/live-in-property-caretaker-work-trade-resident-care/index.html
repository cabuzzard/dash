Live-In Property Caretaker — Work-Trade & Resident Care

2026-10-22

A property that runs itself while you're away isn't a fantasy — it's what happens when the right person is already there. This is a selective, individually scoped live-in caretaking arrangement for Northern California property owners who need more than a house-sitter and less than a property management company. One person, embedded in the property, handling the daily work — with every term in writing before occupancy begins.

Who This Is For

Property owners in Northern California who have more property than they can actively manage and no reliable person on the ground. Time-poor professionals, part-time residents with a second home sitting empty between visits, hobby farm and ranch owners whose animals and land require daily presence, and retirees whose physical capacity no longer matches the demands of the place they own.

You are not looking for a corporation or a platform. You are looking for one specific, known person you can hand a key to — and stop thinking about it.

What's Included

A live-in caretaking arrangement structured to the actual property and negotiated individually. What that covers:

- Routine home and grounds maintenance — seasonal yard work, small repairs under California's ~$1,000 unlicensed threshold, monitoring of home systems, early problem detection before small issues become expensive ones. - Animal care where agreed — on the schedule the animals actually need, regardless of where you are. - Property watch through owner travel — not camera-monitored remotely, not checked on occasionally. Present, on-site, making judgment calls as they come up. - Written agreement before move-in — duties, weekly hours, housing terms, utilities, compensation structure (paid, accommodation-based, or a lawful combination), trial period, and explicit exit terms. Nothing left as an informal understanding. - Referral-first intake — you speak to someone who has already handed over a key before you do.

How It Works

The process: property and accommodation inquiry → mutual fit discussion → documented duties, time, housing, and compensation → references and legal review as needed → signed agreement before occupancy. No one moves in without a written scope. That's the mechanism, not a formality.

The arrangement begins with a defined trial period — typically four to eight weeks — before any long-term term is established. Both sides need to see the arrangement in operation. After that, the long-term occupancy and ongoing scope are confirmed in writing.

Why It Works

Most informal caretaking arrangements break down the same way: the scope was never written down, the exit was never discussed, and the occupancy status was ambiguous. This arrangement is built around the documentation that prevents exactly that. Duties, hours, legal occupancy terms, and exit notice are explicit from the start — which protects the owner legally and removes the ambiguity that turns a good idea into a conflict.

The other reason it works: this is not a stranger from an app. The intake is referral-preferred. The trust is established before the agreement is signed, not assumed afterward. An embedded, known presence — someone who already knows where the water shutoff is, what the animals eat, and what that sound from the crawlspace means — is a different category of solution than anything a platform can send you.

The Main Objection Answered

"I don't know you."

That's the point of the referral-first intake. You speak to someone who has already handed over a key — who can tell you what the scope looked like in practice, how long the arrangement ran, and how the exit was handled — before you make any decision. The trust is built before the agreement is signed. And the agreement itself covers what happens if it doesn't work out: exit terms are written in, notice periods are defined, and occupancy ends cleanly. There is no ambiguity about how it ends on either side. That is the whole structure.

On legality: a work-trade arrangement — accommodation as compensation for labor — is lawful in California when duties, hours, and occupancy terms are documented. That documentation is part of what this arrangement includes, not something you have to figure out afterward. And on repair scope: jobs under approximately $1,000 in combined labor and materials that do not require a permit are legal for an unlicensed person in California. The scope is clear. Everything inside it gets handled; everything outside it gets referred to a licensed contractor.

Terms and Next Step

Compensation is paid, accommodation-based (housing in exchange for defined labor), or a lawful combination — set at the time of agreement, matched to the actual scope of the property. Price and structure are determined individually; there is no fixed rate because no two properties are the same. Duration and exit terms are written and explicit.

Availability is limited and intake is selective. Arrangements are verified individually — duties, legal terms, and occupancy conditions reviewed before anything is signed.
