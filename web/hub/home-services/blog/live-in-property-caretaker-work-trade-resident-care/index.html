Live-In Property Caretaker — Work-Trade & Resident Care

2026-10-22

A property that runs itself while you're away isn't a fantasy — it's what happens when the right person is already there.

You know the feeling. You leave town and spend the first two days of the trip half-thinking about the place — whether the irrigation held, whether the animals are on schedule, whether that slow drip under the sink became something worse while no one was there to catch it. You've tried coordinating a pet sitter, a house checker, and a contractor who never called back. You've tried apps that send strangers with no stake and no toolkit. None of it quite covers the gap.

This is a selective, individually scoped live-in caretaking arrangement for Northern California property owners who need more than a house-sitter and less than a property management company. One person, embedded in the property, handling the daily work — with every term in writing before occupancy begins.

The Problem Isn't the Property. It's the Gap.

Contractors won't return calls for jobs under a few thousand dollars. The small stuff piles up — the fence latch, the faucet, the irrigation fitting — until it isn't small anymore. House-sitting apps send someone to watch and wait, which is fine until something actually needs handling. Property managers coordinate paperwork and vendor calls from a distance; they do not feed the horses at 6am or notice the slow drip before it becomes a water-damaged ceiling.

The gap between licensed contractor and do-it-yourself is real, and nothing currently fills it. That's the lane this arrangement occupies.

What Changes When Someone Capable Is Already There

The property stays maintained — not checked on occasionally, not monitored remotely through a camera feed, but actively managed by a known person who already knows where the water shutoff is, what the animals eat, and what that sound from the crawlspace means. Small repairs get caught before they compound. The yard stays on schedule. The animals don't miss a feeding because you're in a different time zone.

You stop managing logistics from a distance. Your attention can actually leave the property when you do.

What the Arrangement Actually Covers

Every arrangement is scoped individually to the property — no two are the same. What that typically includes:

Routine home and grounds maintenance — seasonal yard work, early problem detection, monitoring of home systems, and small repairs within California's unlicensed handyman threshold (jobs under approximately $1,000 in combined labor and materials that do not require a permit). That covers a meaningful slice of the small stuff that contractors won't return calls for. Anything outside that threshold gets referred to a licensed professional; the scope is clear and works inside it.

Animal care where agreed — on the schedule the animals actually need, not the schedule that's convenient for someone checking in.

Property watch through owner travel — on-site presence and active judgment, not remote monitoring. Someone who can make a call when something comes up, not someone who has to Google the address to find the water shutoff.

A written agreement before anyone moves in — duties defined in plain terms, weekly hours, housing and utility terms, compensation structure (paid, accommodation-based, or a lawful combination), a trial period, and explicit exit terms. Nothing left as an informal understanding between two people who had good intentions.

Referral-first intake — you speak to someone who has already handed over a key before you decide anything.

How It Gets Structured

Property and accommodation inquiry. A conversation about mutual fit. Documented duties, weekly time commitment, housing terms, and compensation — matched to the actual scope of the property, not a fixed rate. References and legal review where needed. A signed agreement before occupancy begins.

The arrangement starts with a defined trial period — typically four to eight weeks — before any long-term term is established. Both sides need to see the arrangement in operation before committing. After that, the ongoing scope and occupancy are confirmed in writing.

No one moves in without a written scope. That's the mechanism, not a formality.

Why the Documentation Is the Whole Point

Most informal caretaking arrangements break down the same way: the scope was never written down, the exit was never discussed, and the occupancy status was ambiguous. California tenant law is not indifferent to occupancy — an arrangement without clear written terms defining the basis of occupancy can create rights neither side intended. The documentation that prevents this is part of what this arrangement includes from the start, not something to sort out after the fact.

A work-trade structure — accommodation as compensation for defined labor — is lawful in California when duties, hours, and occupancy terms are documented. That documentation is standard here, not an afterthought.

The Objection You're Already Thinking

"I don't know you."

That's exactly why the intake is referral-first. You speak to someone who has already handed over a key — who can tell you what the scope looked like in practice, how long the arrangement ran, how the exit was handled, and whether the person showed up when no one was watching. The trust is built before the agreement is signed, not assumed after.

And the agreement itself covers what happens if it doesn't work out. Exit terms are written in. Notice periods are defined. Occupancy ends cleanly, with no ambiguity about timing or terms on either side. The structure is what makes it safe to say yes.
