Care Command

One person owns the whole board — appointments, referrals, authorizations, provider communication — so you stop being the unpaid care administrator and go back to being family.

Who it's for: Adult children managing a parent's care alongside a full-time life, and seniors navigating a care system that has more moving parts than hours in the week

What's included

  • Initial fit conversation to establish whether this is the right engagement
  • Full needs and goals review — conditions, providers, medications, open gaps, family priorities
  • A written care plan: every provider, every open action item, one owner per task
  • Ongoing coordination — referral tracking, authorization follow-up, provider communication, appointment prep
  • Scheduled check-ins so the picture stays current and nothing sits unowned between visits

Why it works

Decision paralysis happens in the theoretical space you make up when the information is scattered and no one owns the follow-up. A care manager collapses that space — one accountable person with the contacts, the procedural knowledge, and the organized system to keep every moving part on a single visible track. That is not emotional support; it is operational clarity.

The objection it answers

'We can handle this ourselves' — you have been handling it, and that is exactly the problem. The question is not whether you are capable; it is whether you have the hours and the system contacts to do it without something slipping. Most families find out the hard way that they do not, and reactive decisions made under pressure cost more than a planned engagement ever would.

Terms

Planned engagement — not a crisis hotline. Scope, pricing, and any clinical service boundaries are being defined and will be published transparently before enrollment opens. Medicare and insurance coverage applicability will be clearly explained so you know exactly what you are committing to.
