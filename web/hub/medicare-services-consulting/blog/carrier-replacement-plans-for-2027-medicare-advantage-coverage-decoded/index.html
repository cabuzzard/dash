Carrier Replacement Plans for 2027 Medicare Advantage Coverage Decoded

2026-10-26

Your carrier's letter says a replacement plan will be available. That sentence is doing a specific job — and the job is not to protect your coverage. A significant number of Medicare Advantage members receiving plan-discontinuation notices right now are being moved toward a crosswalk plan chosen for the carrier's financial sustainability, not for your doctors, your drug list, or your out-of-pocket reality. The window to make an active choice closes December 31, 2026.

The news in brief

For the 2027 plan year, a significant number of Medicare Advantage members are receiving carrier notices that their current plan is being discontinued. CMS requires carriers to offer a crosswalk replacement plan and to notify affected members before October 15, 2026. Members who do not actively enroll in a different plan by December 31, 2026 are auto-enrolled into whatever crosswalk plan the carrier has selected.

What happened

Every fall, CMS requires carriers to notify members whose plans will not continue into the following year. That notice triggers a Special Enrollment Period running through December 31 — a window that allows affected members to choose a different Medicare Advantage plan, switch to Original Medicare with a standalone Part D drug plan, or in most states apply for a Medigap supplement policy. According to the pillar content sourced for this post, carriers are required under CMS guidelines to select a crosswalk plan that is 'similar' to the discontinued plan and within the same service area. 'Similar' is a regulatory standard. It refers to plan type and geography — not to individual provider contracts, formulary tier placements, prior authorization requirements, or out-of-pocket maximums. The carrier's discontinuation letter names the crosswalk plan and typically includes a phone number connecting members to the carrier's own sales team. It is not required to disclose whether specific physicians are contracted with the new plan, whether maintenance drugs have moved to a higher formulary tier, or whether the new plan's out-of-pocket maximum is higher than the one being discontinued.

Why it matters

Auto-enrollment is not a good fit — it is the administrative default. The crosswalk plan was selected because it is financially sustainable for the carrier. That means it may carry higher cost-sharing at the point of care, a narrower network to reduce claims exposure, or a formulary structured to limit expensive drug tiers. Any one of those features can cost you real money in 2027 without your noticing the shift until you are at a specialist's office or at the pharmacy counter. A drug moving from Tier 2 to Tier 4 can shift your monthly cost by forty to one hundred dollars or more. Multiply that by twelve before you decide the plan is 'similar.' A higher out-of-pocket maximum — which has run above eight thousand dollars in recent years at the CMS-set limit — is not abstract if you use your coverage regularly. Prior authorizations you currently hold may not carry over, meaning a potential gap in ongoing treatment, not just paperwork. None of those gaps are disclosed in the carrier's letter. They are not required disclosures. The letter satisfies the regulatory notice requirement. It does not satisfy your informational need. That gap is where bad coverage decisions happen.

Our perspective

The mechanism behind this problem is direct: when a carrier discontinues a plan, it is making a business decision — the plan is no longer financially viable at the premium and benefit level offered. The replacement it selects is, by definition, a plan built around the carrier's economics. It was not built around your value set. Treating the crosswalk plan as a neutral default is the same as letting the carrier make your coverage decision for you. The comparison that actually protects your 2027 coverage is not between the crosswalk brochure and your memory of what you currently have. It is between the new plan's actual 2027 formulary, provider directory, and cost-sharing schedule — checked against your real drug list, your real doctors, and your real usage pattern from the past twelve months. That comparison takes two to three hours of careful document work if you do it yourself. Medicare.gov tells you what plans exist and what their rated features are. It does not know your cardiologist's name, your insulin brand, or how many specialist visits you had this year. Closing that gap is the work that actually determines what 2027 costs you. If you work with someone, make sure that person has no financial stake in which plan you choose. Carrier-paid agents have a ceiling on how clearly they can explain a comparison when one option pays them and another does not. A fee-based Medicare consultant — paid by you for the hour, not by the carrier for the enrollment — can run that comparison with no stake in the outcome.

What happens next

The Special Enrollment Period window closes December 31, 2026. Before it does, you can enroll in a different Medicare Advantage plan in your area, switch to Original Medicare with a standalone Part D plan, or in most states apply for a Medigap policy (underwriting rules vary by state and guaranteed-issue rights are not universal in this window). After December 31, your options narrow significantly. Members who miss the window can make further changes during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period from January 1 through March 31, 2027 — but they will have spent at minimum one month in a plan they did not choose. Two open questions worth watching: provider contracts for 2027 are not always finalized until after open enrollment closes, so a specialist confirmed in-network in October may not be contracted by January — call the provider's billing office directly rather than relying on online directories. And while CMS protections do require continuity-of-care allowances if a provider leaves a network mid-year and an exception process if a drug is removed from a formulary mid-year, those protections require you to invoke them. You have to know they exist first.
