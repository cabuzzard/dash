Medicare Clarity Consulting

2026-10-22

You are not confused because you're unsophisticated. You are confused because every source you've found so far — the comparison websites, the toll-free numbers, the friendly mailers — is funded by the carriers who want you to pick their plan. A Medicare Clarity Consultation is the one hour in this process where nobody in the room has a carrier contract. You get a structured, plain-English conversation built around your situation, and you leave with a written roadmap that tells you exactly what to do next.

Who this is for

Adults approaching 65 or recently enrolled in Medicare — often within a 6-to-24-month window of their Initial Enrollment Period — who have hit a wall of jargon, conflicting advice, and agents who won't say how they get paid. Working seniors navigating secondary-payer rules. People with a complex drug list who need someone to pull the actual formulary, not just confirm the carrier offers drug coverage. Anyone who has spent three browser tabs deep on Medicare.gov and still isn't sure what applies to them. If you're financially literate and accustomed to making your own decisions, but Medicare is making you feel like you're missing something — you are not. The system is deliberately opaque, and this session is built to make it legible.

What's included

A 60-minute consultation by phone or video with a Medicare expert who holds no carrier contracts and earns no commission from any plan you choose. A written one-page personal Medicare roadmap delivered within 48 hours of your session — not a brochure, not a follow-up sales email, but a documented decision framework built around your providers, your drug list, your work status, your income, and your enrollment timeline. Plain-language explanations of which Parts apply to your situation, what each enrollment window means for you specifically, and what the real cost difference between your plan options looks like in dollars. No upsell at the end. No follow-up sales contact unless you initiate it. If you book the Plan Match and Enrollment Review within 30 days, the consultation fee is credited in full.

How it works

You book the session and pay the flat fee at checkout. Before the call, you'll share the basics: your current coverage, your doctors, your prescriptions, your work situation, and your timeline. On the call, a Medicare expert maps your specific situation — not the average situation, yours — and walks you through what the decision actually means in your life. After the call, your written roadmap arrives within 48 hours. It names your options, explains the trade-offs in plain language, and tells you what to do next. You can act on it immediately, hand it to a broker, or use it to make sense of anything else you read. The roadmap is yours.

Why it works

Most Medicare confusion is not a knowledge problem — it's a structural problem. Every free resource in this category has a financial relationship with a carrier, which shapes what gets recommended and what gets left out. This session breaks that structure. The fee is flat and paid directly to the advisor; nothing changes based on what plan you choose. That's not a marketing position — it's a different business model, and it's the only one in this category that lets the advice be genuinely independent. The mechanism is a one-to-one diagnostic built around your value set: your providers, your risk tolerance, your timeline, your real costs. General information tells you what exists. This tells you what it means for you.

The objection it answers

*"I can get free advice from a broker."* You can — and that broker gets paid by the carrier whose plan you enroll in. CMS caps Medicare Advantage commissions at $611 for a new enrollment. That is not a conflict of interest you can reason around; it is a structural incentive built into every conversation with a carrier-paid agent. Free advice with a hidden commission structure is not free. It is a sales call. This session is the cost of advice that has no stake in your outcome. One wrong enrollment decision — a missed window, an incorrect secondary-payer assumption, a plan that doesn't cover your specialist — can cost thousands in permanent penalties or uncovered care. The consultation fee is small against that number.
