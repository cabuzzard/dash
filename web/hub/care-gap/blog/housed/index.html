Why Housing Stability Is Essential to Keeping Caregivers in the Workforce

2026-10-22

You are working — often more than one job — and still behind on rent. The eviction notice arrived while you were on a double shift. The system around you was built to help the person you care for, not you. This program exists to change that. Keeping Caregivers Housed is a structured housing stability and navigation pathway built specifically around the income structures, documentation gaps, and time constraints that define care work. It is not a referral list. It is not a generic emergency fund. It is a purpose-built intervention that treats your housing crisis as what it is: a workforce emergency — and responds accordingly.

Who this is for

Direct care workers, home health aides, certified nursing assistants, and unpaid family caregivers whose housing is at risk because the work of keeping someone else alive has gutted their own financial stability. You are disproportionately likely to be a woman, a woman of color, earning wages that were never designed to absorb a missed paycheck or a broken lease. You are not between jobs. You are working, and still behind. You have burned through every informal safety net you had — family, friends, credit — before asking for help. You do not have time to navigate a system that was not designed for you. And you are afraid that asking for help might cost you your job, your clients, or the last bit of ground you are standing on.

What's included

A structured intake and assessment that maps your actual situation — income, housing status, eligibility triggers, timeline — rather than running you through a generic checklist that treats your agency employment or irregular hours as disqualifying.

Active navigation, not a list of phone numbers. Someone who moves the process with you — who knows which programs are currently funded and accepting applications, who anticipates the documentation problems before they cause a denial, and who treats your time as the scarce resource it is.

Connection to funded, active emergency rental or housing assistance programs where you meet eligibility criteria. Referrals in this pathway go to programs that are live and funded right now — not programs that closed six months ago or have a twelve-week backlog.

Coordination with caregiver services you may already be connected to — so this is not a siloed transaction that disappears after one check clears. It sits inside a broader support infrastructure.

A documented record of your stabilization — not just for you, but one that exists beyond this single crisis and contributes to the workforce data that drives future funding and expanded program access.

How it works

You come in through a structured intake — by phone, online, or through a coordinating caregiver service. The intake is built to read care work: irregular agency income, 1099 contractor arrangements, multiple employers, documentation gaps. What disqualifies you from a generic program is context here, not a red flag. From intake, you are assessed against active funded programs your situation actually fits. A navigator moves the application process with you — prepping documentation, solving the eligibility problems in advance, and connecting to a program on a timeline measured in days, not months. Where emergency direct assistance is available and you meet criteria, it moves. Outcomes are recorded at the case level so your stabilization becomes part of a documented record that supports program reporting and future funding.

Why it works

Generic emergency rental assistance treats a home health aide exactly like any other low-income applicant. That means the irregular pay, the agency employment structures, and the documentation gaps that define care work become disqualifying — even when the income is real and the need is acute. This program understands care work from the inside. It was built to contextualize those features, not penalize them. The math is direct: the cost of one stabilization intervention is a fraction of the cost of replacing one caregiver — and the caregiver in front of you is fully employed, already committed to the person she serves, and one paycheck from an exit that looks like a voluntary quit in every data system that tracks it. The intervention does not have to be large. It has to be timely, accessible, and built around your actual situation. That is what this program delivers.

The objection it answers

"I've tried this before and nothing came through."

Prior failed applications through generic programs are exactly why this pathway exists. Standard programs were designed around W-2 income from a single employer. They were not designed around care work. When a home health aide earning through two agency placements submits an application with income documentation that does not fit the form, the application fails — not because she does not qualify, but because the intake process does not know how to read her. This program's navigation is built specifically to find the angle that works given care work's documentation and income profile. If you have been denied before, that is not evidence that you do not qualify. It is evidence that you were in the wrong intake system.

Terms and next step

This is a direct-service program delivered at no cost to the caregiver. Entry is through a structured intake and assessment. Navigation and referrals are provided at no cost. Emergency direct assistance is available where a funded program is active and eligibility is confirmed through that program's own process. Your housing situation is not reported to your employer, your agency, or your clients — this program operates independently of your employment relationship. Coordination with existing caregiver services is built into delivery where you are already connected to them.

To get started, complete the intake assessment. A navigator will be in contact to walk the process with you from there. You do not need to have your documentation ready before you begin — that is what the navigation is for.
