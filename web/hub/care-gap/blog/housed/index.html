Why Housing Stability Is Essential to Keeping Caregivers in the Workforce

2026-10-22

You are working — often more than one job — and still behind on rent. The eviction notice arrived while you were on a double shift. The system around you was built to help the person you care for, not you.

This program exists to change that. Keeping Caregivers Housed is a structured housing stability and navigation pathway built specifically around the income structures, documentation gaps, and time constraints that define care work. It is not a referral list. It is not a generic emergency fund. It is a purpose-built intervention that treats your housing crisis as what it is: a workforce emergency — and responds accordingly.

You have been showing up for everyone except yourself

Direct care workers, home health aides, and unpaid family caregivers are disproportionately women, disproportionately women of color, earning wages that were never designed to absorb a missed paycheck or a broken lease. You are not between jobs. You are working, and still behind. You have burned through every informal safety net — family, friends, credit — before getting here. You do not have time to navigate a system that was not designed for you. And you are afraid that asking for help might cost you your job, your clients, or the last bit of ground you are standing on.

That fear makes sense. The system was not built around you. This program was.

The programs you applied to before were not built for care work

Standard rental assistance programs verify income the way a landlord reads a W-2 — consistent weekly paystubs from a single employer. A home health aide earning through two agency placements, with hours that fluctuate week to week, submits documentation that does not fit the form. The application fails — not because she does not qualify, but because the intake process does not know how to read her.

That is not a personal failure. That is a design flaw in a system that was never built around care work income structures. Prior failed applications are not evidence that you do not qualify. They are evidence that you were in the wrong intake system.

Housed, assessed, and connected to programs that are actually funded

What you get here is not a list of phone numbers. It is a navigator who moves the process with you.

The intake is built to read care work: irregular agency income, 1099 contractor arrangements, multiple employers, documentation gaps. What disqualifies you from a generic program is context here — not a red flag. From intake, your actual situation is assessed against funded programs that fit it. A navigator prepares the documentation, solves the eligibility problems before they become denials, and connects you to assistance on a timeline measured in days, not months.

Where emergency rental or housing assistance is available and you meet eligibility, it moves. Your stabilization is documented at the case level — a record that exists beyond this single crisis and contributes to the workforce data that supports future program funding and expanded access.

A clear picture of what you qualify for — assessed, not guessed.

Someone who handles the navigation so you are not doing this alone at midnight after a double shift.

Access to funded, active programs — referrals in this pathway go only to programs that are live and accepting applications right now, not programs that closed six months ago.

Coordination with caregiver services you may already be connected to — so this is not a siloed transaction that disappears after one check clears.

A documented record of your stabilization that supports future funding and policy, not just this moment.

One well-timed intervention changes everything downstream

The math on this is not complicated. Replacing one home health aide costs between $2,500 and $5,500 in recruitment, screening, onboarding, and the coverage gaps that open while that position sits empty. A housing stabilization intervention that keeps that worker employed costs a fraction of that — and the caregiver in front of you is fully employed, already committed to the person she serves, and one paycheck from an exit that looks like a voluntary quit in every data system that tracks it.

The intervention does not have to be large. It has to be timely, accessible, and built around your actual situation. That is what this program delivers.

What you are probably thinking right now

"I probably don't qualify." The assessment exists specifically because standard eligibility criteria were not built around care work income structures. You do not know what you qualify for until someone who understands your employment situation walks it through with you.

"I don't have time to apply for anything." Navigation is the program. You are not handed a list. Someone moves the process with you.

"This will just refer me somewhere that can't actually help." Referrals go only to funded, active programs. If a program is not live and funded, it is not in the referral pathway.

"Asking for help might affect my job." This program operates independently of your employer. Your housing situation is not reported to your agency or your clients.

How to get started

This is a direct-service program delivered at no cost to you. Entry is through a structured intake and assessment — by phone, online, or through a coordinating caregiver service. You do not need to have your documentation ready before you begin. That is what the navigation is for.

Complete the intake assessment. A navigator will be in contact to walk the process with you from there. You deserve to be on solid ground — not because you are done giving care, but because keeping you housed is what makes it possible to keep giving it.
