Give a Caregiver a Roof

2026-10-22

Most campaigns in this space fund the person receiving care. This one funds the person making care possible. When an unpaid caregiver loses housing stability — because they left the workforce, reduced their hours, or spent every margin of their income holding someone else's life together — the care arrangement collapses with it. This is the only donor-facing funding pathway built specifically for that moment. One-time gifts. Recurring monthly giving. Foundation and corporate partnership tracks. Transparency before the ask, not after it.

Who This Is For

This is for donors who already understand the problem from the inside — caregivers themselves, former caregivers, family members of care recipients who watched someone burn through savings and stability to show up every day without a paycheck or a break. It is also for foundation program officers and corporate CSR teams looking for a documented, underserved gap their capital can actually close — one with no dominant grantee, no crowded donor field, and a measurable intervention at the center.

You are not looking for a cause to feel good about. You are looking for one you already know is real, with a mechanism you can verify before you commit.

What's Included

Your gift — at any tier — routes through a clearly identified nonprofit organization and reaches a defined set of caregiver housing stability services:

- Rental assistance bridging the gap between reduced caregiver income and monthly housing costs - Transitional housing support for caregivers whose arrangement ended and left them without a place to return to - Accommodation support tied to caregiving placement, where proximity to the care recipient comes with its own cost - Organizational transparency — legal name, EIN, 501(c)(3) documentation, program budget allocation, and third-party rating — presented before any payment prompt, not buried afterward - Impact documentation — named housing outcomes, disbursement timelines, and verification sources so you know exactly what your dollar did

One-time gifts at any amount receive immediate confirmation and a clear breakdown of how funds are applied. Recurring monthly giving is framed in concrete terms: what a sustained minimum threshold actually sustains for a defined unit of service. Foundation and corporate partners receive a dedicated intake process, an impact reporting cadence, and co-branding options where appropriate.

How It Works

Before any ask is made, you see who holds the funds, what percentage reaches direct services, what housing stability looks like as a measurable outcome, and what independent verification exists. That is not a courtesy — it is the baseline. A donor who already knows this problem is real does not need to be sold the pain. They need to trust the pathway.

The gift moves through a single verified payment mechanism. The organization is identified by legal name and tax ID. The program budget discloses direct service percentage versus administrative overhead. Named housing outcomes — number of caregivers housed, average duration of housing stability, geographic reach — are present before the donation button, not in a follow-up email.

Why It Works

Housing instability is not a peripheral issue for caregivers. It is the mechanism that forces them out of caregiving entirely. When a caregiver cannot sustain their own housing, they cannot sustain the care arrangement — which means the aging parent, the disabled spouse, the person relying on a family member to hold the system together suddenly has no one. Every dollar that holds a caregiver's floor in place holds everything above it too.

This funding pathway owns a gap that no Medicare-adjacent campaign and no general caregiver nonprofit has claimed: donor-facing, caregiver-centered, housing-anchored. When enough individual floors stop collapsing, the system stops operating in emergency mode. That is where measurable impact actually lives — not in a single gift, but in the recurring floor that replaces the patch.

The Objection This Answers

"I already give to elder care charities — isn't that the same thing?"

It is not. Elder care funds the person receiving care — their medical costs, their Medicare supplemental coverage, their living arrangements. This funds the person making care possible. A gift to an elder care nonprofit does nothing for the caregiver who is losing their own housing while holding the care arrangement together. These are not the same gap. One side of the structure is well-funded. The other wall is actively collapsing, and this is the only campaign watching it.

If your concern is verification — whether the organization is legitimate, whether your money reaches direct services — that information is present before the payment prompt. Legal name, tax ID, public filings, third-party rating links. You do not have to take anyone's word for it.
