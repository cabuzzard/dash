Finding Support When Caregiving Becomes Unsustainable

2026-10-22

You are not a patient. You do not have a diagnosis. What you have is a schedule that leaves no room for you, and a weight that has been building long enough that you have stopped being able to name it. This community exists because 53 million caregivers are navigating a system designed entirely around the person they are caring for — and not one piece of it was built for them. That ends here.

Who This Is For

This is for family caregivers — most of them women, most of them unpaid — who arrived somewhere inside Medicare research and realized, quietly, that they are falling apart. It is for the caregiver in their 40s, 50s, or 60s who is still working, still raising a family, and somehow also managing coverage decisions, appointment schedules, and medication logistics for someone else, while their own health slides and their own appointments go unscheduled.

It is also for professional and direct care workers — home health aides, personal care workers, CNAs — whose job is caregiving and whose workplace offers nothing to support the human doing that work. You carry the same load. You qualify for the same support. This community is built for anyone whose life or job is caregiving, paid or unpaid.

If you have hit the wall — the place where caregiving stops feeling like love and starts feeling like suffocation — this is the place that sees you first, before asking anything of you.

What's Included

Peer connection with people living the same week you are living. Not a hotline. Not a referral to a professional you have to explain everything to from scratch. People who already know what your schedule looks like, because theirs looks the same.

Curated resource referrals — named and reachable. Respite care options. Financial assistance programs caregivers actually qualify for. Counseling resources. Assistance programs most caregivers have never been told exist. Surfaced and organized so you do not have to spend the hours you do not have finding them yourself.

Education on the specific mechanics of caregiver life. Burnout recognition. Boundary-setting. System navigation. The hard conversations caregiving requires — with family members, with providers, with yourself. Not general wellness content. Content built for the situation you are actually in.

Emotional support built around you — not the person you support. The community is structured to validate the experience before offering tools. You will not be asked to be grateful or strong. You will be seen, and then you will be given something useful.

An ongoing space, not a program with an end date. This is not a six-week course. It is a place caregivers return to — when things get harder, when something changes, when they need to find their footing again.

How It Works

Access is low-barrier and the core community is free. You find the community, you find people who are living what you are living, and you find your way to resources — in the same space, without having to navigate multiple agency websites or explain your situation to a succession of intake forms.

The community operates in the margins of the schedule you already have. You do not add this to your list. You replace the isolation and the endless solo searching with something that has already done that work for you.

As the community grows, additional resources — structured peer groups, expert access, a curated resource library — will be added. What is available right now is stated plainly below. Nothing is promised that is not already built.

Why It Works

Peer support reduces isolation. Reduced isolation directly reduces the cognitive and emotional tax of caregiving — this is a documented mechanism, not a soft benefit. When you stop carrying something alone, the weight does not disappear, but your capacity to carry it increases. That is the practical outcome this community is designed to produce.

The second mechanism is navigation. Most caregivers do not know what they qualify for. Respite care programs, state Medicaid waiver stipends, National Family Caregiver Support Program services, VA Caregiver Support Programs for those caring for veterans — these exist, they are funded, and they go unused because no one has pointed caregivers at them directly. This community does exactly that.

The Objection This Answers

"I do not have time for one more thing."

This is not one more thing. It is the place you go so you stop doing all of it alone. The research burden that has been entirely yours — finding resources, identifying programs, figuring out what exists and whether you qualify — that stops being yours alone to carry the moment you are inside this community. The isolation breaks on day one. The time you spend here is not added to your load. It replaces the hours you were spending searching alone and coming up empty.

If you have tried a support group before and it did not help, it is worth naming why: most groups are organized around a diagnosis or a grief stage, not around the specific mechanics of caregiver life — the scheduling, the guilt, the system navigation, the identity erosion. This community is different in structure, not just in tone. It is built around where you actually are, not around the clinical category of the person you care for.

And if you are a professional caregiver wondering whether this space is for you: yes. Directly and without qualification. The emotional and logistical weight of paid caregiving is the same. The support is accessible to you in the same spaces.

Terms and Next Step

Core access to the Caregiver Support Community is free. Peer connection, resource referrals, and caregiver education content are available at no cost. Optional expansions — paid membership tiers, expert Q&A, and a structured resource library — will be added as they become available and will be priced and described plainly when they are live. No features are promised that are not already built.

To join, use the button below. You will land in a space where the problem has already been named, people are already here who know what your week looks like, and the first resource referral is closer than any search you have done on your own. You do not have to solve all of this today. You have to take one step — and this is the right one for where you are right now.
