Construction Project Intelligence: Spot Budget, Schedule and Risk Problems Early

2026-10-22

You already have the data. Job cost reports, schedule updates, committed costs, change order logs — it's all sitting somewhere. The problem isn't access. It's that nobody is reading it on a cadence that matches how construction actually moves, and by the time the numbers tell you something is wrong, the margin you needed to fix it is already gone. Construction Project Intelligence is a recurring managed analysis service that pulls your active project data every reporting cycle, runs it through AI-assisted pattern recognition, and returns a plain-language management report: where each job stands, where it's trending, and what needs a decision this week.

Who It's For

Mid-size general contractors and specialty subcontractors running two to fifteen active projects simultaneously. Owner-operators and project executives who built their businesses on field instinct and are now managing enough volume that gut feel alone is leaving money on the table. You're not a CFO. You don't have a controller watching every line item. You're running crews, chasing RFIs, and trying to read your own financials at 9pm on a Tuesday — and you know something is off before you can prove it. This service is for the contractor who's competent enough to recognize the gap and ready to close it.

What's Included

- Recurring management reports — delivered monthly or bi-weekly per active project, or as a portfolio-level summary across all jobs, depending on your scope - Cost-to-complete analysis — your actual spend, committed costs, and change order exposure read together against your original budget, so the forecast reflects reality, not optimism - Schedule-to-cost alignment — the comparison most contractors never run: whether your cost burn rate and your schedule completion rate are moving at the same speed, and what it means when they're not - Risk indicator review — aging RFIs, open change order backlog, subcontractor billing pace, and owner decision dependencies tracked every cycle, before they become cost or schedule impacts - Plain-language action items — not raw numbers, not a data dump: specific decisions and escalations that need to happen before the next reporting cycle - Priority off-cycle analysis — retainer clients get fast turnaround when a job goes sideways between reporting windows

How It Works

Onboarding pulls your existing cost data and establishes a baseline — no new software required on your end. You don't change how you work. Every reporting cycle, the actual project data comes in: budgets, costs to date, committed costs, schedule status, change order exposure. It gets run through AI-assisted analysis built to catch the patterns a human checking spreadsheets once a month will miss — cost code drift that looks manageable in isolation, contingency burn rates that signal scope assumption problems, float consumption rates that reveal schedule optimism before the end date moves. What comes back is a management report in contractor language: health status per job, portfolio view across your whole book, and a short list of what needs to happen before the next cycle. Engagements are structured as a project fee for a single job or a monthly retainer scaled to project count.

Why It Works

The mechanism is pattern recognition across cost codes and schedule milestones running on a cadence that matches how construction actually moves. Standard monthly job cost reports describe what happened. This service reads the leading indicators — committed cost exposure, labor productivity drift, change order backlog age, subcontractor billing pace — that show up weeks before the lagging financial signals do. That's the window where problems are still fixable. A 2% margin variance on a $3M job is $60,000. Caught at 40% completion, it's a recoverable adjustment. Caught at closeout, it's an absorbed loss. The difference is not smarter people — it's the same data, read earlier, by a process that doesn't skip cycles when things get busy.

The Objection It Answers

"I already have project management software."

Your software captures data. This analyzes it and tells you what it means. Those are different jobs, and conflating them is exactly why contractors who have Procore or Buildertrend or a Sage job cost module are still manually assembling financial narratives the night before an owner meeting. The software did its job. The analysis job isn't getting done. This service is the analysis layer — the part that sits between your data and the decision you need to make this week.

And if your concern is the cost: one caught overrun on one job, caught early enough to act on, covers this service for a year. The question isn't whether you can afford it. It's whether you can afford what you currently can't see.
